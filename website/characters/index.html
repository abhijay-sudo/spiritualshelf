The antagonists

Those who made the story hard, as the stories themselves tell them.

  • Ravana रावणLord of Lanka: a great scholar of the Veda and, tradition holds, among the greatest of Shiva’s devotees.
  • Shurpanakha शूर्पणखाDaughter of the sage Vishravas, sister of Ravana, whose desire and whose wound set the war of Lanka in motion.
  • Kaikeyi कैकेयीThe queen who once bore the wounded Dasharatha from battle, and whose two boons sent Rama to the forest.
  • Manthara मंथराKaikeyi’s lifelong maid, called Kubja, whose counsel turned a queen’s joy into Rama’s exile.
  • Shakuni शकुनिSon of Subala and brother of Queen Gandhari: a chariot-warrior of Gandhara whom none could match at dice.
  • Duryodhana दुर्योधनEldest of Dhritarashtra’s hundred sons: a Kuru prince, master of the mace, the friend who made Karna a king.
  • Hiranyakashipu हिरण्यकशिपुSon of Kashyapa, king of the Daityas, whose tapas scorched the worlds and who conquered all three.
  • Meghanada मेघनादRavana’s mighty son, the warrior Brahma himself named Indrajit: he who conquered Indra.
  • Jayadratha जयद्रथKing of the Sindhus and son-in-law of Dhritarashtra, whose severe penance won a boon from Lord Shiva.
  • Mahishasura महिषासुरLord of the asuras, who fought the gods for a hundred years, conquered them all and reigned as Indra.
  • Kamsa कंसThe mighty king of the Bhojas, who so feared his sister’s eighth child that he saw the Lord in every breath.
  • Holika होलिकाIn later tradition and the festival of Holi, the king's sister whom fire could not burn.
  • Surapadman सूरपद्मनSon of Kashyapa and Maya, whose ten-thousand-year sacrifice won from Shiva a thousand and eight universes to rule.
  • Daksha दक्षLord of the Prajapatis, Brahma’s son and Sati’s father, whose great sacrifice at Kanakhala left out Shiva.
