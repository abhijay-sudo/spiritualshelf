Also in the stories

  • Narada नारदDevarshi Narada, sage of the gods and knower of the three worlds, who gave Valmiki the story of Rama.
  • Lakshmana लक्ष्मणRama’s younger brother and second self, who chose exile at his side over a palace.
  • Vishvamitra विश्वामित्रThe king who became a brahmarshi by tapas alone; guru to Rama, and by tradition seer of the Gayatri.
  • Janaka जनकKing of Mithila, revered as a rajarshi, who found Sita in the earth and gave her hand to Rama.
  • Bharata भरतThe brother who was handed a kingdom and gave it back, ruling fourteen years in the name of Rama’s sandals.
  • Guha गुहKing of the Nishadas at Shringaverapura, the friend Rama held as dear as his own self.
  • Shabari शबरीThe ascetic of Matanga’s forest, perfected in tapas, whose lifelong waiting ended the day Rama came.
  • Jatayu जटायुKing of vultures and old friend of Dasharatha, who gave his life trying to save Sita.
  • Mainaka मैनाकThe golden mountain hidden beneath the sea, who rose to offer Hanuman rest in mid-flight.
  • Vishnu विष्णुThe Lord who sustains the worlds and descends in age after age to protect them.
  • Ganga गंगाThe river of heaven, purifier of the worlds. “Among rivers I am Ganga,” says Krishna in the Gita.
  • Bhagiratha भगीरथThe royal sage of Ikshvaku’s line, forefather of Rama, whose penance brought Ganga down to earth.
  • Suratha सुरथThe king of Chaitra’s line who ruled the whole earth and guarded his people like his own children.
  • Samadhi समाधिThe merchant the Goddess calls best of his kind: offered any boon, he asked only for knowledge.
  • Parvati पार्वतीDaughter of Himavan, revered by the worlds, whose fierce tapas won her Shiva.
  • Dharma धर्मRighteousness itself, honoured as a god: father of Yudhishthira, whom he tests three times.
  • Indra इंद्रKing of the gods and wielder of the thunderbolt, lord of heaven and father of Arjuna.
  • Shantanu शांतनुKuru king of Hastinapura, husband of Ganga and father of Bhishma, hailed by all kings as King of Kings.
  • Satyavati सत्यवतीThe fragrant ferry-maiden of the Yamuna who became queen of the Kurus, and mother of the sage Vyasa.
  • Bhishma (Devavrata) भीष्म (देवव्रत)Son of Shantanu and Ganga, grandsire of the Kurus, whose terrible vow of renunciation gave him his name.
  • Drona द्रोणSon of the sage Bharadwaja, master of the Vedas and of arms, and teacher of the Kaurava and Pandava princes.
  • Arjuna अर्जुनSon of Kunti by Indra, peerless archer of the Pandavas, to whom Krishna spoke the Bhagavad Gita.
  • Ekalavya एकलव्यSon of the Nishada king Hiranyadhanus, who made a clay image his teacher and mastered the bow by devotion.
  • Draupadi द्रौपदीBorn from the sacrificial altar of King Drupada: princess of Panchala and queen of the five Pandavas.
  • Vidura विदुरBrother and minister of the Kuru kings, whom the epic calls the god of justice born on earth.
  • Bhima भीमSecond of the Pandavas, son of the Wind god, named at birth the foremost of all the strong.
  • Dhritarashtra धृतराष्ट्रThe blind king of the Kurus, son of Vyasa, mighty as ten thousand elephants, whose eye was wisdom.
  • Karna कर्णSon of Surya and Kunti, born in divine armour: the archer-king of Anga who sent no brahmana away empty-handed.
  • Surya सूर्यThe Sun, thousand-rayed maker of day, called Vivasvat; father of Karna and of Yama.
  • Kunti कुंतीPritha, a Yadava princess raised by King Kuntibhoja; mother of Karna and of the three elder Pandavas.
  • Sanjaya संजयSon of Gavalgana and minister of King Dhritarashtra, to whom Vyasa gave divine sight to see the whole war.
  • Prahlada प्रह्लादThe asura prince the Bhagavata calls a mahabhagavata. “Among Daityas I am Prahlada,” says the Gita.
  • Narasimha नृसिंहVishnu as man-lion, who came out of a pillar to make his devotee’s word true.
  • Sugriva सुग्रीवSon of the Sun and king of the vanaras, whose friendship, sworn before fire, gave Rama an army.
  • Vali वालीSon of Indra and king of Kishkindha, so mighty that he once carried Ravana under his arm across the oceans.
  • Dasharatha दशरथKing of Ayodhya in Ikshvaku’s line, ally of the gods in war, who died of grief for the son he had to exile.
  • Sumantra सुमंत्रDasharatha’s charioteer and counsellor, of whom Rama said: the Ikshvakus have no truer friend.
  • Vibhishana विभीषणRavana’s righteous brother, who asked Brahma only that his mind never leave dharma, even in danger.
  • Jambavan जाम्बवानAncient king of the bears, born of Brahma, whose words woke Hanuman to his own strength.
  • Kartikeya कार्तिकेयSix-faced son of Shiva, general of the gods. “Among commanders I am Skanda,” says the Gita.
  • Suniti सुनीतिQueen of Uttanapada and mother of Dhruva, whose counsel set her son on the path to Vishnu.
  • Nanda नंदGreat-hearted chief of the cowherds of Vraja, in whose house Krishna grew up as a son.
  • Vasishtha वसिष्ठBrahmarshi and guru of the Ikshvaku kings, who gave Rama his name and, at the end, his crown.
  • Abhimanyu अभिमन्युSon of Arjuna and Subhadra, nephew of Krishna: the young hero who broke into the wheel array alone.
  • Savitri सावित्रीPrincess of the Madras, born of a goddess's boon, whose wisdom and constancy won her husband back from Death.
  • Satyavan सत्यवानPrince of the Shalvas, raised in a forest hermitage; named Satyavan for the truthfulness of his parents.
  • Yama यमSon of Vivasvat and lord of the departed, called Dharmaraja because he deals one law to all beings.
  • Vamana वामनVishnu born to Aditi as a dwarf brahmachari, who measured the universe in two strides.
  • Bali बलिPrahlada’s grandson, lord of the three worlds, whom the Bhagavata counts among the twelve who know dharma.
  • Shukracharya शुक्राचार्यSage of Bhrigu’s line, guru of the asuras. “Among seers I am Ushanas,” says Krishna in the Gita.
  • Yashoda यशोदाNanda’s wife, the cowherd woman of Gokula who took the Lord of all the worlds for her own small son.
  • Balarama बलरामRohini’s son and Krishna’s elder brother, named Rama for the joy he gives and Bala for his strength.
  • Brahma ब्रह्माThe creator of the worlds, grandfather of all that lives, who grants the fruit of every tapas.

The antagonists

Those who made the story hard, as the stories themselves tell them.

  • Ravana रावणLord of Lanka: master of the Vedas, mighty in tapas, and honoured by tradition as a great devotee of Shiva.
  • Shurpanakha शूर्पणखाDaughter of the sage Vishravas, sister of Ravana, whose desire and whose wound set the war of Lanka in motion.
  • Kaikeyi कैकेयीThe queen who once bore the wounded Dasharatha from battle, and whose two boons sent Rama to the forest.
  • Manthara मंथराKaikeyi’s lifelong maid, called Kubja, whose counsel turned a queen’s joy into Rama’s exile.
  • Shakuni शकुनिSon of Subala and brother of Queen Gandhari: a chariot-warrior of Gandhara whom none could match at dice.
  • Duryodhana दुर्योधनEldest of Dhritarashtra's hundred sons: a Kuru prince, master of the mace, the friend who made Karna a king.
  • Hiranyakashipu हिरण्यकशिपुSon of Kashyapa, king of the Daityas, whose tapas scorched the worlds and who conquered all three.
  • Meghanada मेघनादRavana’s mighty son, the warrior Brahma himself named Indrajit: he who conquered Indra.
  • Jayadratha जयद्रथKing of the Sindhus and son-in-law of Dhritarashtra, whose severe penance won a boon from Lord Shiva.
  • Mahishasura महिषासुरLord of the asuras, who fought the gods for a hundred years, conquered them all and reigned as Indra.
