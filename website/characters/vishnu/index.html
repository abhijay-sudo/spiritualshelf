Gods & Puranas

Vishnu

विष्णु

Shri Hari · Narayana

Vishnu, Hari and Narayana, is worshipped as the Lord who sustains the worlds and descends to protect them age after age. As Vamana he took back heaven in two strides; he became the arrow with which Shiva burned three cities. As a boar he sought the foot of Shiva’s pillar of fire, found none, and said so; Shiva gave him worship equal to his own. He came on Garuda to the child Dhruva, touched him with his conch, and gave him the pole star.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Vishnu was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Title: he is called Hari and Narayana in these stories. Bhagavata Purana 8.23.13 (harir nārāyaṇaḥ)
  2. Title: he is called Hari and Narayana in these stories. Bhagavata Purana 4.9.4 (hariḥ)
  3. The one Lord takes the name Hari for the sustaining of the world. Bhagavata Purana 1.2.23 (sthity-ādaye hari-viriñci-hareti saṁjñāḥ)
  4. His descents protect the worlds age after age. Bhagavata Purana 1.3.28 (mṛḍayanti yuge yuge)
  5. Comes on Garuda to Dhruva in Madhuvana, touches him with his conch, grants the pole star. Bhagavata Purana 4.9.1–4, 4.9.20–21
  6. Promises Aditi he will be born as her son; appears from her and takes the form of Vamana. Bhagavata Purana 8.17.18
  7. Promises Aditi he will be born as her son; appears from her and takes the form of Vamana. Bhagavata Purana 8.18.1, 8.18.12
  8. As Vamana asks three paces; two strides cover earth and heaven; heaven is returned to Indra. Bhagavata Purana 8.19.16
  9. As Vamana asks three paces; two strides cover earth and heaven; heaven is returned to Indra. Bhagavata Purana 8.20.32–34
  10. As Vamana asks three paces; two strides cover earth and heaven; heaven is returned to Indra. Bhagavata Purana 8.23.19
  11. When the gods report Mahishasura’s conquest, he and Shiva grow angry; a great light comes from his face and from the bodies of the other gods, gathers into one and becomes the Goddess. Devi Mahatmya 2 = Markandeya Purana 82.8–12 (Sanskrit text)
  12. When the gods report Mahishasura’s conquest, he and Shiva grow angry; a great light comes from his face and from the bodies of the other gods, gathers into one and becomes the Goddess. Markandeya Purana, Canto 82, tr. F. E. Pargiter (1904)
  13. Descends as Narasimha: appears from the pillar to make his servant’s word true. Bhagavata Purana 7.8.17–18 (also in Narasimha’s entries)
  14. Descends as Rama: the gods ask Vishnu to be born as Dasharatha’s son; Rama is born a portion of Vishnu. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 15.19–31, 18.8–11 (also in Rama’s entries)
  15. Descends as Krishna: the Govardhana verse names Krishna Vishnu. Bhagavata Purana 10.25.19 (also in Krishna’s entries)
  16. Becomes the arrow of Shiva’s chariot that burns the three cities. Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, Yuddha Khanda 8.26; 10.27
  17. As a boar he searches for the root of the column of fire, finds none, returns exhausted and tells the truth; Shiva, pleased that he kept to truth, gives him a footing equal to his own, with his own temples, festivals and worship. Shiva Purana, Vidyeshvara Samhita 7.16–18, 7.26, 7.31–33
  18. The child Dhruva, five years old, does tapas in the forest of Madhuvana; by the fifth month he stands on one leg, motionless as a pillar (also in Dhruva’s entries). Bhagavata Purana 4.8.42, 4.8.76; 4.12.23 (pañca-varṣaḥ)
