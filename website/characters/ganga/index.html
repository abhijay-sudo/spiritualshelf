Ganga

गंगा

Bhagirathi · Tripathaga

Ganga, river of heaven and Himavan’s elder daughter, is revered as purifier of the worlds, Tripathaga, flowing through all three. Drawn by Bhagiratha’s penance, she fell meaning to sweep Shiva into the underworld; he held her in his hair for years, then freed her in seven streams.

Names

Ganga. Also known as: Bhagirathi, Tripathaga.

Where this comes from

What this page about Ganga was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Brahma names her Tripathaga and Bhagirathi. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 44.5–6
  2. Krishna: among rivers I am Jahnavi (Ganga). Bhagavad Gita 10.31 (srotasām asmi jāhnavī)
  3. Elder daughter of Himavan and Mena; Uma is the second daughter; called purifier of the worlds, best of rivers, river of the gods. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 35.14–18, 35.22, 35.24
  4. Falls from the sky onto Shiva’s head; wanders many years in his matted hair; released toward Bindu lake in seven streams. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.4–13
  5. The seventh stream follows Bhagiratha’s chariot; Jahnu drinks her and releases her, hence Jahnavi; she reaches the ocean and the ashes of Sagara’s sons, who attain heaven. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.14–15, 43.34–41
  6. Sixty thousand sons of Sagara delivered. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 44.3
  7. As she falls she resolves to seize Shiva with her current and enter the netherworld; Shiva, knowing her pride, resolves to make her vanish. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.5–6
  8. Sages and gandharvas touch the water fallen from Shiva’s body as holy; those cast down from heaven by curses bathe in it, are cleansed and return to their worlds. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.26–28
