Gods & Puranas

Krishna

कृष्ण

Giridhari · Yogeshvara

Krishna, whom the Bhagavata calls Bhagavan himself, is worshipped as Vishnu come to earth: born to Devaki, raised in Nanda’s Vraja. As a child he let Yashoda bind him when no rope would reach.

Read more

At seven he held Govardhana over all Vraja for seven days. At Kurukshetra he taught Arjuna the Gita: “Whenever righteousness decays, I come forth … age after age.” He showed him a form bright as a thousand suns.

Study dossierArchetype, turning point and teaching, for those who want to go deeper

Archetype & symbolism

The friend who is also the teacher. Krishna stands for play and counsel together: the flute in and the charioteer’s voice at Kurukshetra belong to one figure.

The defining moral crucible

Arjuna lowering his bow between two armies. Krishna answered despair not with comfort but with a way of acting without clinging to results.

Core teaching

Your right is to the action, never to its fruit.

तुम्हारा अधिकार कर्म पर है, उसके फल पर कभी नहीं।

Names

Krishna. Also known as: Giridhari, Yogeshvara, Govinda, Kanha, Madhava.

Also written: Govind, Madhav.

Where this comes from

What this page about Krishna was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Giridhari (‘bearer of the hill’) is the name tradition gives him for lifting Govardhana; the deed itself is in the Bhagavata. Traditional epithet; the deed is narrated in Bhagavata Purana 10.25.19, 10.25.23
  2. The Gita calls him Yogeshvara, lord of yoga. Bhagavad Gita 18.78 (yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇaḥ)
  3. The Bhagavata calls Krishna Bhagavan himself. Bhagavata Purana 1.3.28 (kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam)
  4. Born of Devaki; carried by Vasudeva to Nanda’s Vraja and laid in Yashoda’s bed. Bhagavata Purana 10.3.8, 10.3.47, 10.3.51
  5. Worshipped as Vishnu himself: the Govardhana verse names him Vishnu. Bhagavata Purana 10.25.19 (dadhāra līlayā viṣṇuḥ)
  6. Lifted Govardhana with one hand, as a child holds a mushroom; held it seven days without moving; sheltered Vraja from Indra’s storm. Bhagavata Purana 10.25.2 (the Samvartaka clouds), 10.25.15–23
  7. He was seven years old; he is called the breaker of Indra’s pride. Bhagavata Purana 10.26.3, 10.26.14 (sapta-hāyano bālaḥ), 10.26.25 (mahendra-mada-bhit)
  8. Indra anoints him and names him Govinda. Bhagavata Purana 10.27.22–23
  9. Drives Arjuna’s chariot between the two armies. Bhagavad Gita 1.21–22
  10. Drives Arjuna’s chariot between the two armies. Bhagavad Gita 1.24
  11. His teaching there is the Bhagavad Gita (for example, the right to action and not to its fruit). Bhagavad Gita 2.47
  12. Every rope two fingers short; seeing his mother’s weariness he lets himself be bound. Bhagavata Purana 10.9.15–18 (also in Yashoda’s entries)
  13. The universal form: if a thousand suns rose at once in the sky, that might resemble its splendour. Bhagavad Gita 11.12
  14. “Whenever dharma declines and adharma rises, I manifest myself … age after age.” Bhagavad Gita 4.7–8
