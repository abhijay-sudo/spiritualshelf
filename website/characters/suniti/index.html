Gods & Puranas

Suniti

सुनीति

Dhruva-janani

Suniti, the queen King Uttanapada loved less, is the mother whose counsel set Dhruva on the path to Vishnu. When her five-year-old came to her stung by her rival Suruci’s words, she took him on her lap, burning with grief herself, and told him to wish no one ill and seek only the lotus-eyed Lord.

Read more

When he came home, she held him close and forgot all her sorrow. Rising at last to his star, Dhruva thought of his mother, and saw her going on ahead of him.

Names

Suniti. Also known as: Dhruva-janani.

Where this comes from

What this page about Suniti was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. The Bhagavata calls her Dhruva’s mother (jananī). No separate honorific epithet is traditional for her; the title is this designation from the text. Bhagavata Purana 4.12.32 (sunītiṁ jananīṁ dhruvaḥ)
  2. One of Uttanapada’s two queens; Suruci was dearer to the king; Dhruva is her son. Bhagavata Purana 4.8.8
  3. Takes the weeping Dhruva on her lap; herself in tears; tells him to wish no one ill and to worship the lotus-eyed Lord, the only one who can end his sorrow. Bhagavata Purana 4.8.15–23
  4. Narada: the path your mother told you is the path to your highest good. (Suruci had named Narayana first, as a taunt, at 4.8.13; the line and text credit Suniti with the counsel Dhruva followed.) Bhagavata Purana 4.8.40
  5. At his ascent Dhruva remembers his mother and is shown her going ahead of him. Bhagavata Purana 4.12.32–33
  6. She burns with grief as if in a forest fire when she hears what happened. Bhagavata Purana 4.8.16
  7. On his return she embraces her son, dearer than life, and forgets all her grief. Bhagavata Purana 4.9.49–50
