Ramayana

Jambavan

जांबवान

Riksharaja

Jambavan, ancient king of the bears, is remembered as the elder whose words woke Hanuman to his strength. Born, Brahma says, from his own yawn, he had in his youth walked around Vishnu as the god took his three strides.

Read more

Old now, and unable to leap the whole ocean, he told Hanuman the story of his birth and of the child who sprang at the sun, and called on him to rise. Hanuman rose and leapt; from his returning roar Jambavan knew that Sita was found.

Names

Jambavan. Also known as: Riksharaja.

Where this comes from

What this page about Jambavan was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Brahma: “I created Jambavan, foremost of bears, long ago; he came forth from my mouth as I yawned” Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 17, verse 7
  2. Oldest of the company; can now leap only ninety yojanas; in his youth he walked around Vishnu Trivikrama at Bali’s sacrifice Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda, Sarga 65, verses 10–17
  3. Rouses Hanuman with the story of his birth and childhood: “Arise, leap this ocean” Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda, Sarga 66, verses 1–37
  4. Hearing Hanuman’s roar: he has succeeded, or he would not sound like this Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda, Sarga 57, verses 22–23
  5. Checks Angada’s plan to storm Lanka: first see what Rama wishes Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda, Sarga 60, verses 1–6
  6. Called king of the bears (riksharaja); with Sushena and many bears he guards the rear of the marching army Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 4, verses 20 and 34
