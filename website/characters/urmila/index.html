Ramayana

Urmila

उर्मिला

Yashasvini · Priyadarshana

Urmila, Janaka’s daughter, is remembered for the fourteen years she spent apart from Lakshmana while he served Rama in the forest. Her father gave her with Sita, saying it three times; bright as flame, she circled the fire with Lakshmana. In exile Sita spoke of her: my younger sister, virtuous, lovely to behold. Valmiki is silent on her waiting; Maithili Sharan Gupt wrote Saket chiefly so she would not stay poetry’s neglected one.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Urmila was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Second daughter named after Sita; given to Lakshmana; the gift affirmed three times: Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 1.71.20-22 (sītāṃ rāmāya ... ūrmilāṃ lakṣmaṇāya; dvitīyām ūrmilām; trir vadāmi / trir dadāmi). Sources of the story “Sita Ram Vivah”
  2. At the foot of the altar with the other brides, bright as flames: 1.73.15 (Janaka says "my daughters", plural, of all the brides). Sources of the story “Sita Ram Vivah”
  3. Janaka calls Lakshmana to accept Urmila and take her hand: 1.73.30-31. The couples circle the fire: 1.73.35-39. Sources of the story “Sita Ram Vivah”
  4. Called yaśasvinī (illustrious) when the queens of Ayodhya receive the brides: 1.77.11-12 (sarga 77 is the cited source of ramayana-parashurama, page 9). Sources of the story “Sita Ram Vivah”
  5. Title: "Yashasvini" is the epithet of 1.77.11; "Lakshmana-patni" (wife of Lakshmana) is a plain description, not a quoted epithet. Sources of the story “Sita Ram Vivah”
  6. Left out: the later, regional tradition of Urmila sleeping through the fourteen years of exile is not in these sargas and is not used. Sources of the story “Sita Ram Vivah”
  7. For the title 'Priyadarshana' and 'my younger sister': in the forest, telling Anasuya of her wedding, Sita says her father himself gave 'my younger sister, the virtuous Urmila, lovely to behold' (mama anujā sādhvī ūrmilā priyadarśanā) to Lakshmana as wife. Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 2.118.53 (critical edition 2.110.51); Anasuya's response follows at 2.111.1 (CE)
  8. For the title 'Yashasvini': the queens of Ayodhya receive 'Urmila, the illustrious' (ūrmilāṃ ca yaśasvinīm). Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 1.77.11 in valmikiramayan.net numbering (1.77.12 at IIT Kanpur)
  9. The exile is for fourteen years (nava pañca ca varṣāṇi; caturdaśa varṣāṇi). Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 2.11.26 and 2.40.12
  10. Lakshmana asks to go as Rama's attendant (anucara): to walk ahead with his bow, fetch roots and fruit, and do everything while Rama wakes or sleeps. Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 2.31.22-25
  11. Only Rama, Lakshmana and Sita mount the chariot and leave Ayodhya ('the three, Sita the third', sītātṛtīyān); Urmila is not among them, so she stays behind. Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 2.40.13-17
  12. Valmiki is silent on her waiting: in a full-text search of the critical edition (all seven kandas), Janaka's daughter Urmila is named only at the betrothal and wedding (CE 1.70.21-22, 1.71.3, 1.72.18; vulgate 1.71-1.73), the homecoming (CE 1.76.9; vulgate 1.77.12) and in Sita's words to Anasuya (CE 2.110.51; vulgate 2.118.53); CE 1.32.12 is a different Urmila, mother of the gandharvi Somada. Nothing is said of her parting from Lakshmana or of the fourteen years. Valmiki Ramayana, critical edition e-text, GRETIL (searched for 'ūrmil')
  13. Urmila is remembered for her fourteen years apart from Lakshmana: Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi's essay laments that Valmiki and Tulsidas give no word to her parting and calls her giving up her husband to Rama and Sita, soon after her wedding, a self-sacrifice (ātmotsarga) through fourteen years of separation. Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi, Rasajna Ranjan, essay 8, 'Kaviyon ki Urmila-vishayak udasinata' (public domain)
  14. Maithili Sharan Gupt wrote Saket mainly so that Urmila would not remain 'kavya ki upekshita' (poetry's neglected one); two whole cantos (9 and 10) are given to her separation. Ramchandra Shukla, Hindi Sahitya ka Itihas, Adhunik Kaal, Kavya Khand, Prakaran 2 'Nai Dhara', section on Maithili Sharan Gupt (public domain)
  15. Stayed in Ayodhya: Valmiki does not say where she spent the fourteen years. She is received into Ayodhya with the other brides (1.77.12) and is not among the three who leave the city by chariot (2.40.16, sītātṛtīyān); Shukla notes that Saket, named for Ayodhya, describes chiefly what happens in Ayodhya and gives two whole cantos (9 and 10) to her separation, and Dwivedi pictures her in the royal palace as Lakshmana leaves for fourteen years. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 1.77.12 and Ayodhya Kanda 2.40.16 (IIT Kanpur numbering); Ramchandra Shukla, Hindi Sahitya ka Itihas, Adhunik Kaal, Kavya Khand, Prakaran 2, on Saket; Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi, Rasajna Ranjan, essay 8
