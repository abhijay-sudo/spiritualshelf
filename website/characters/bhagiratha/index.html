Gods & Puranas

Bhagiratha

भगीरथ

Rajarshi

Bhagiratha, the royal sage from whom Rama descends, brought Ganga down to earth where three kings before him had failed. After thousands of years of penance he stood a year on one toe, until Shiva agreed to bear her fall. When she vanished into Shiva’s hair, he began his penance again. Behind his chariot she reached the ashes of Sagara’s sons, who rose to heaven. Tradition calls a tireless effort a Bhagiratha effort.

ShivaRamayana, Bala Kanda

Ganga Avataran: The river in Shiva's hair

A king stands a year on one toe. Heaven's river leaps to sweep away the god who catches her, and is lost for years in his locks, until one stream turns to follow a chariot down to the ashes of the dead.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Bhagiratha was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Title: Valmiki calls him rajarshi (royal sage). Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 42.11 (bhagīrathas tu rājarṣiḥ)
  2. Title: Valmiki calls him rajarshi (royal sage). Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.14, 43.30
  3. Son of Dilipa; of the Ikshvaku line. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 42.7, 42.20–22
  4. Rama descends from him (Dilipa, Bhagiratha, Kakutstha, Raghu … Dasharatha, Rama). Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 70.38–43
  5. Entrusts the kingdom to his ministers; long penance at Gokarna for thousands of years; Brahma grants the boon and says only Shiva can bear Ganga. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 42.11–24
  6. Stands a year on the tip of his big toe; Shiva agrees to bear Ganga on his head; Ganga follows his chariot to the ocean and to the ashes of his ancestors, who attain heaven. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.1–3, 43.14–15, 43.39–41
  7. Sixty thousand sons of Sagara freed; Ganga named Bhagirathi after him; Sagara, Anshuman and Dilipa before him could not accomplish it. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 44.3, 44.5–6, 44.8–12
  8. ‘Bhagiratha effort’ (Bhagiratha prayatna; in Hindi, Bhagiratha prayas) for a tireless undertaking is later proverbial usage, marked as tradition in the text; Valmiki’s basis is Brahma’s praise of what he alone accomplished. Later tradition; compare Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 44.8–13
  9. When Ganga does not come out of Shiva’s hair, he takes up great penance again. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.9 (Heritage IIT Kanpur numbering; 43.10 on sa.wikisource)
