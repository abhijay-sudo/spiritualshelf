Mahabharata

Drona

द्रोण

Dronacharya · Son of Bharadwaja

Dronacharya, teacher of the Kuru princes, was born of the sage Bharadwaja, mastered the Vedas, and received every weapon of Parashurama. He promised Arjuna no archer would equal him and, reminded of it, asked Ekalavya for his thumb.

Read more

As Kaurava commander he formed the wheel array that closed on Abhimanyu. Told, falsely, that his son had fallen, he laid down his weapons and sat in yoga, and passed, the epic says, to the realm of Brahman.

Where this comes from

What this page about Drona was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Drona springs from Bharadwaja’s seed kept in a vessel (drona) and studies all the Vedas and their branches; the son of Bhrigu (Parashurama) gives him all his weapons and the whole science of arms. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXXI
  2. Drona teaches the Kuru and Pandava princes; promises Arjuna that no archer in the world shall equal him; refuses Ekalavya and later asks his thumb as fee. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXXIV
  3. Arjuna outdoes the other pupils at the test of the bird. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXXV
  4. Drona is installed as commander of the Kaurava forces. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Drona Parva, Section VII
  5. “The preceptor… had formed the great circular array.” Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Drona Parva, Section XXXII
  6. Drona tells the Kaurava warriors that Abhimanyu cannot be vanquished bow in hand, and that they must deprive him of his car and bow. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Drona Parva, Section XLVI
  7. Arjuna reminds Drona: “Thou hadst lovingly told me… that no pupil of thine should be equal to me”; Drona reflects, takes Arjuna to Ekalavya and asks his thumb. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXXIV
  8. Told that Aswatthaman is dead (Yudhishthira adding “elephant” indistinctly), Drona yields to despair. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Drona Parva, Section CXCI
  9. “Laying aside his weapons then in that battle, and sitting down on the terrace of his car, he devoted himself to Yoga”; “the preceptor attained to the supreme region of Brahman”. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Drona Parva, Section CXCIII
About these sources
  • Section CXCIII: CXCII in other numberings.
