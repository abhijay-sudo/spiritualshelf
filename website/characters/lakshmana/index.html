Ramayana

Lakshmana

लक्ष्मण

Saumitri

Lakshmana, son of Sumitra, is remembered as the brother who would not be parted from Rama; Valmiki calls him Rama’s life-breath walking outside his body, and from childhood Rama would neither sleep nor eat without him. Tradition reveres him as Shesha incarnate.

Read more

He would not stay behind in Ayodhya, guarded Rama and Sita through the exile, and slew Indrajit in the war. At Rama’s coronation, pressed to be crown prince, he declined, and Rama gave it to Bharata.

Names

Lakshmana. Also known as: Saumitri.

Not to be confused with Lakshmi, the goddess of fortune.

Where this comes from

What this page about Lakshmana was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Devoted to Rama from childhood; “like another life-breath outside him”; Rama would not sleep or eat without him Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 18, verses 28–31
  2. Insists on going to the forest and offers to serve Rama and Sita waking and sleeping Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 31, verses 1–8 and 22–26
  3. Washes Rama’s feet and stands watch while Rama and Sita sleep; Guha keeps vigil with him Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 50, verses 49–50
  4. Consecrates Vibhishana with water from the sea at Rama’s word Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 19, verses 24–26
  5. Slays Indrajit with the weapon of Indra Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 90, verses 66–77
  6. Declines the office of crown prince though pressed; Rama then consecrates Bharata Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 128, verses 93–94
  7. An incarnation of Shesha Tradition, not the cited text: held by later Vaishnava tradition; not stated in the chapters cited here, and the retelling words it as tradition.
