Ramayana

Kausalya

कौसल्या

Rama-janani

Kausalya is one of King Dasharatha’s three queens. When he resolves on the horse sacrifice for a son, he tells his queens to take the vow, and their faces brighten.

Read more

When a being rises from the fire with the gods’ payasa, the king goes straight to Kausalya and gives her half. Six seasons after the sacrifice, in the twelfth month, Chaitra, on its ninth day, under Punarvasu with Cancer rising, she gives birth to Rama, and Valmiki says she shone with that son as Aditi did with Indra, wielder of the thunderbolt.

Names

Kausalya. Also known as: Rama-janani.

Where this comes from

What this page about Kausalya was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. One of Dasharatha’s three queens: 1.15.20 (the gods ask Vishnu to be born of his three wives, bhāryāsu tisṛṣu); 1.16.26-29 (she is named first among the queens given the payasa). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  2. The queens told to take the vow; their faces brighten like lotuses at the end of the cold season: 1.8.23-24 (the queens are not named individually there). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  3. The king goes into the women’s quarters, straight to Kausalya, and gives her half the payasa: 1.16.26-27 (kausalyāyai narapatiḥ pāyasārdhaṃ dadau). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  4. Gives birth to Rama when six seasons had passed after the sacrifice, in the twelfth month, on the ninth tithi of Chaitra (ṛtūnāṃ ṣaṭ samatyayuḥ, tataś ca dvādaśe māse; the verse does not say from when the twelve months are counted), under the star whose deity is Aditi (Punarvasu), with Cancer rising and Jupiter rising with the moon: 1.18.8-11 (one numbering of this passage; others differ by a verse). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  5. Shone with her son as Aditi with Indra, the vajra-wielder: 1.18.12 (the IIT Kanpur text numbers it 1.18.11). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  6. The title “Rama-janani” (mother of Rama) is a plain descriptive compound resting on 1.18.10 (kausalyā ajanayad rāmam), not a quoted epithet. Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
About these sources
  • Kausalya’s ritual part with the sacrificial horse and her night beside it (1.14.33-34) is left out of the telling; her later story in the Ayodhya Kanda is not told here and is not claimed.
