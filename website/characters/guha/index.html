Ramayana

Guha

गुह

Nishadaraja

Guha is king of the Nishadas, ruling at Shringaverapura on the Ganga; Valmiki calls him a friend as dear to Rama as his own self. When Rama arrives in exile, Guha comes on foot to embrace him and offers his whole kingdom, but Rama, bound by his vow, accepts only fodder for his father’s horses.

Ram Vanvas: Fourteen years, two sandals

On the eve of his crowning, Rama is sent to the forest. Sita and Lakshmana follow, Guha keeps watch by the Ganga, and Bharata climbs to Chitrakuta to give back a throne he never wanted.

Where this comes from

What this page about Guha was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Rama halts near Shringaverapura on the Ganga Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 50, verse 26
  2. “A king named Guha, a friend equal to Rama’s own self, a Nishada by birth”; lord of the Nishadas Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 50, verses 33–35
  3. Comes on foot with ministers and kinsmen, embraces Rama, offers food, beds and his whole land Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 50, verses 34–41
  4. Rama accepts only fodder for the horses Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 50, verses 43–47
  5. Keeps vigil over Rama through the night, bow in hand, with Lakshmana Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 50, verses 49–51
  6. Has a boat brought at dawn; his kinsmen row Rama, Sita and Lakshmana across Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 52, verses 5–10 and 74–81
