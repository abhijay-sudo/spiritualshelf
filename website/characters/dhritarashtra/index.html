Dhritarashtra

धृतराष्ट्र

Prajnachakshu · King of the Kurus

Dhritarashtra, blind king of the Kurus, is called by the epic Prajnachakshu, he whose eye is wisdom: son of Vyasa and Queen Ambika, foretold a learned royal sage with the might of ten thousand elephants. Yet love for Duryodhana overruled what he knew was right.

Read more

As the dice fell he kept asking, “Has the stake been won?” Offered sight for the war, he would not watch his kin slain, and heard it all, and the Gita, from Sanjaya.

Names

Where this comes from

What this page about Dhritarashtra was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Vyasa foretells that the son of the eldest princess (Amvika) “shall be equal in might unto ten thousand elephants… an illustrious royal sage, possessed of great learning”, but blind. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CVI
  2. The citizens speak of Dhritarashtra as “possessed of the eye of knowledge” though born blind. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXLIII
  3. “The monarch endued with the eye of wisdom” (Sanskrit prajnachakshus). Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Udyoga Parva, Section LX
  4. Dhritarashtra, glad at heart, “asked repeatedly, ‘Hath the stake been won?’” and could not conceal his emotions. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Sabha Parva, Section LXIV
  5. Vidura warns him during the game; the game continues. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Sabha Parva, Section LXI
  6. He grants Draupadi boons freeing Yudhishthira and his brothers. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Sabha Parva, Section LXX
  7. Vyasa offers him sight for the battle (“I will, O son, grant thee vision”); he answers, “I like not to behold the slaughter of kinsmen”, and wishes to hear of it; Vyasa gives Sanjaya celestial vision to narrate it to him. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Bhishma Parva, Section II
  8. Sanjaya concludes the Gita before the king. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Bhishma Parva, Section XLII
  9. Yudhishthira to Sanjaya: “For such a son as this, king Dhritarashtra knowingly abandoned virtue and pleasure.” Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Udyoga Parva, Section XXVI
