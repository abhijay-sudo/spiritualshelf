Gods & Puranas

Himavan

हिमवान

Himachala · Shailaraja

Himavan, lord of the mountains, is Mena’s husband, Parvati’s father, and father of many sons, Mainaka the eldest. In the Shiva Purana he asks Narada whose wife Parvati will be, brings her to serve Shiva on the ridge of Gangavatarana, and goes to her forest to call her home from her tapas. Turned for a while against the match, he is won back by the seven sages and the counsel of Meru and the other mountains, and gives his daughter to Shiva with the words “to the great Rudra”.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Himavan was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Name and title: Himavan / Himavat, also Himachala (chapter colophons and Shastri's English throughout); "shailaraja", king of mountains (Sanskrit 36.14). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  2. Line / about: husband of Mena and father of Parvati (6.5-7, 6.42); many sons (7.13, 7.18), Mainaka the eldest (21.18, 30.6-7). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  3. About: asks Narada to read Parvati's palm and say whose wife she will be (8.5). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  4. Epic / about: brings Parvati to serve Shiva on the ridge of Gangavatarana (11.4-5, 12.1-5, 13.31-35). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  5. About: goes to her forest to call her home from her tapas (23.2-7). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  6. About: turned for a while against the match (33.4-8, 33.24-27; 32.1-7 for Mena); won back by the seven sages (33.28-37, 36.1-3) and by Meru and the other mountains (36.4-6). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  7. Epic: "all that I have is Shiva's" (36.12). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  8. Epic / about: gives his daughter with the words "tasmai rudraya mahate" and places her hand in Shiva's (48.38-40). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
  9. Nothing in this bio is drawn from outside Rudra Samhita, Parvati Khanda (wisdomlib.org, Shastri translation, paraphrased; Sanskrit checked on sa.wikisource.org). Sources of the story “Parvati's tapas for Shiva”
