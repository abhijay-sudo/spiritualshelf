Gods & Puranas

Holika

होलिका

Sister of Hiranyakashipu

Holika belongs to later tradition and the festival of Holi, not to the Bhagavata Purana, which lists fire among the attempts on Prahlada’s life but tells no story of her. In the festival’s telling she is the sister of the asura king Hiranyakashipu, and fire cannot burn her: some say by a boon, others by a scarf or cloak she wears. She sits in a burning pyre with Prahlada in her lap; she dies in the fire, and he comes out unharmed. Holika Dahan, the bonfire on the night before Holi, is named for her burning.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Holika was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Later tradition, not the Bhagavata: Bhagavata 7.5.43-44 (https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/5/ and the verse page /7/5/43-44/, Sanskrit ‘hima-vayv-agni-salilaih’) lists fire among the attempts with no Holika; she does not appear in 7.4 to 7.10 (all fetched and searched); a vedabase site search for ‘holika’ returns no verse, only a purport at 10.34.20 naming Holika-purnima as a date and one lecture-transcript mention. Wikipedia ‘Holi’, section ‘Background’, places the legend in ‘the 7th chapter of the Bhagavata Purana’; the Bhagavata’s Prahlada chapters do not bear this out. Britannica ‘Holi’, section ‘Myths and stories associated with Holi’ (https://www.britannica.com/topic/Holi) presents it as one of the myths associated with the festival. Sources of the story “Holika Dahan”
  2. Sister of Hiranyakashipu: Britannica ‘Holi’, section ‘Hiranyakashipu and Prahlada’; Wikipedia ‘Holika’, section ‘Legend’; Wikipedia ‘Holika Dahana’, lead. Wikipedia ‘Holika’, Legend also has her offer her help; Britannica has the king enlist it. Sources of the story “Holika Dahan”
  3. Fire could not burn her, by a boon or by a scarf/cloak (tellings differ): Britannica (‘immune to fire’, a protective scarf ‘in some variants’); Wikipedia ‘Holika’, Legend (a boon; variant: her scarf or fireproof garment); Wikipedia ‘Holi’, Background (a cloak). Sources of the story “Holika Dahan”
  4. Sits in a burning pyre with Prahlada in her lap: Wikipedia ‘Holika’, Legend (on her lap atop a burning pyre); Wikipedia ‘Holika Dahana’, lead (on her lap in a bonfire). Britannica has the pyre but does not mention the lap. Sources of the story “Holika Dahan”
  5. She dies in the fire, he comes out unharmed: Britannica (Holika perished; her immunity passed to Prahlada); Wikipedia ‘Holika’, Legend. Sources of the story “Holika Dahan”
  6. Holika Dahan, the bonfire on the night before Holi, named for her burning: Britannica (Choti Holi, the day before Holi; ‘Holika’s Burning’), and its North India section; Wikipedia ‘Holika Dahana’, lead and Significance. Sources of the story “Holika Dahan”
