Ramayana

Narada

नारद

Devarshi

Narada is the devarshi, the sage among the gods: given to tapas and Vedic study, best of the eloquent, knower of the three worlds; tradition pictures him with a veena, always singing of Narayana. When Valmiki asked whether a perfect man lived, Narada named Rama and told his whole life in brief. He set the boy Dhruva on the path to Vishnu, sheltered Prahlada’s mother while the unborn child heard his teaching, and foretold that Satyavan had a year to live.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Narada was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Devoted to austerity and Vedic study, best of the eloquent, foremost of sages; knower of the three worlds Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 1, verses 1 and 6
  2. Called devarshi Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 2, verse 2
  3. Names Rama of the Ikshvaku line and tells his life in brief Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 1, verses 7–8 and following
  4. Finds the boy Dhruva, first counsels patience, then sends him to Madhuvana with a form of Vishnu to meditate on and a twelve-syllable mantra Bhagavata Purana, Skandha 4, Chapter 8, verses 25–62
  5. Makes Indra release Hiranyakashipu’s pregnant queen and shelters her in his hermitage, where the unborn Prahlada hears his teaching Bhagavata Purana, Skandha 7, Chapter 7, verses 7–16
  6. Tells King Ashvapati that Satyavan will die within a year Mahabharata, Vana Parva (Pativrata-mahatmya), Section 292, tr. K. M. Ganguli
  7. Carries a veena and always sings of Narayana Tradition, not the cited text: the familiar picture of Narada in the Puranas and in devotional tradition; not stated in the chapters cited here, and worded in the text as tradition.
