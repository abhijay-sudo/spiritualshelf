Gods & Puranas

Agni

अग्नि

Hutashana · Pavaka

Agni is the god of fire, through whom the gods receive what is offered in the sacrifice. In the Shiva Purana’s Kumara Khanda the gods urge him to take up Shiva’s energy; he does so as a dove, is scorched by it, and begs Shiva for relief.

Read more

It passes from him to six of the sages’ wives, to Himavat, to the Ganga and to the reeds, where it becomes Kumara, whom the text also calls Agni’s son. Agni embraces the child, gives him a shakti, and gives a great shakti again at his crowning.

Names

Agni. Also known as: Hutashana, Pavaka.

Where this comes from

What this page about Agni was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Hutashana (Kumara Khanda 5.45 in the Sanskrit), Pavaka (2.19, 3.19 in the Sanskrit). Sources of the story “Six faces for six mothers”
  2. ‘Through whom the gods receive what is offered’: Kumara Khanda 2.25 (the gods partake of offerings consigned to the fire). Sources of the story “Six faces for six mothers”
  3. Urged by the gods, becomes a dove and takes up Shiva’s energy, swallowing it with his beak (2.10-11; 4.23); scorched, begs Shiva (2.41-44); ‘unbearable’ (2.50). Sources of the story “Six faces for six mothers”
  4. Passes to six of the seven sages’ wives at his flame in Magha (2.54-60); they to Himavat (2.64), Himavat to the Ganga (2.65), the Ganga to the reeds (2.66), where it becomes a boy (2.67). Sources of the story “Six faces for six mothers”
  5. Kumara called ‘son of Agni’ (3.16); Agni calls him son, embraces him, gives the shakti (3.18-19); gives a great shakti to ‘his own son’ at the crowning (5.45). Sources of the story “Six faces for six mothers”
About these sources
  • “God of fire” is the plain meaning of the name; nothing else here is from outside the Kumara Khanda, chapters 2-5.
