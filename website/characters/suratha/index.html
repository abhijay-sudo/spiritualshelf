Gods & Puranas

Suratha

सुरथ

Chaitravanshi Raja · The Manu to Come

Suratha, a king of Chaitra’s line, once ruled the whole earth, guarding his people as his children. Beaten in war, robbed of treasury and army by his own ministers, he rode alone to the hermitage of Medhas; to him and the merchant Samadhi the sage told the Devi Mahatmya. Three years the two worshipped her clay image on a riverbank, offering even their own blood. She promised he would win back his kingdom and be born of the Sun as Manu Savarni.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Suratha was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Title: born of the Chaitra line; promised birth as the Manu Savarni. No separate honorific epithet is traditional for him; the title states these two facts from the text. Devi Mahatmya 1 = Markandeya Purana 81.3 (caitra-vaṁśa-samudbhavaḥ)
  2. Title: born of the Chaitra line; promised birth as the Manu Savarni. No separate honorific epithet is traditional for him; the title states these two facts from the text. Devi Mahatmya 13 = Markandeya Purana 93.14, 93.17
  3. Ruled the whole earth; protected his subjects like his own sons; defeated in war; his ministers seized treasury and army; rode alone on horseback to the forest and came to the hermitage of Medhas. Devi Mahatmya 1 = Markandeya Purana 81.3–10 (Sanskrit text)
  4. Ruled the whole earth; protected his subjects like his own sons; defeated in war; his ministers seized treasury and army; rode alone on horseback to the forest and came to the hermitage of Medhas. Markandeya Purana, Canto 81, tr. F. E. Pargiter (1904)
  5. Savarni, son of the Sun, is the eighth Manu. Devi Mahatmya 1 = Markandeya Purana 81.1–2
  6. Three years of worship before an earthen image; the Goddess grants that he will regain his kingdom within days and, after death, be born of the Sun as the Manu Savarni. Devi Mahatmya 13 = Markandeya Purana 93.7–14, 93.17 (Sanskrit text)
  7. Three years of worship before an earthen image; the Goddess grants that he will regain his kingdom within days and, after death, be born of the Sun as the Manu Savarni. Markandeya Purana, Canto 93, tr. F. E. Pargiter (1904)
  8. The king and the merchant offer bali sprinkled with blood drawn from their own bodies. Devi Mahatmya 13 = Markandeya Purana 93.8 (nija-gātrāsṛg-ukṣitam); tr. Pargiter, Canto 93
