Gods & Puranas

Shiva

शिव

Mahadeva · Gangadhara

Shiva, Mahadeva, great ascetic of Kailasa, is worshipped as the Lord without beginning or end. When Brahma and Vishnu hurled world-burning weapons at each other, he rose between them, a fiery pillar without top or foot; the day they worshipped him he named Shivaratri.

Read more

He took on his head the Ganga the earth could not bear, and burned three cities with one arrow. When Ravana, arms crushed beneath Kailasa, sang to him, Shiva gave him his name and a sword.

  • 116 scenes
  • 115 min read

Study dossierArchetype, turning point and teaching, for those who want to go deeper

Archetype & symbolism

Stillness that holds the river. Shiva is the ascetic and the householder at once: the one who receives Ganga’s fall in his hair so the earth is not broken.

The defining moral crucible

Whether to let Ganga descend with her full force, which would shatter the world, or to receive it. He took the weight himself.

Core teaching

What falls on a steady mind becomes a river, not a flood.

स्थिर मन पर जो गिरता है वह बाढ़ नहीं, नदी बनता है।

Names

Shiva. Also known as: Mahadeva, Gangadhara.

Where this comes from

What this page about Shiva was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Valmiki calls him Mahadeva. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 36.6, 36.9 (mahādeva)
  2. Gangadhara (‘bearer of Ganga’) is the traditional name for the deed Valmiki tells here. Traditional epithet; the deed is narrated in Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.3–9
  3. Lord of Uma, lord of beings (Pashupati), honoured by all the worlds; promises to bear Ganga on his head. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.2–3 (sarvalokanamaskṛtaḥ umāpatiḥ paśupatiḥ)
  4. Called a great ascetic (mahātapāḥ). Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 36.5
  5. Kailasa is his abode. Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, Kumara Khanda 13.9 (tr. J. L. Shastri)
  6. Brahma: the earth cannot bear Ganga’s fall; none but Shiva can hold her. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 42.23–24
  7. Ganga falls on his head; he holds her in his matted hair for many years, then releases her toward Bindu lake, and seven streams arise. Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 43.4–11
  8. He presses the mountain down with his toe when the ten-headed king lifts it; pleased by his hymns of praise, he frees his arms, names him Ravana and gives the sword Chandrahasa. (This sarga calls it the mountain where Shankara sports; tradition, and the story here, name it Kailasa.) Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 16.10, 16.25–27, 16.34–38, 16.44–46
  9. With Parvati sets the condition that whoever circles the earth first is married first; they accept Ganesha’s circling of his parents. Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, Kumara Khanda 19 (tr. J. L. Shastri)
  10. Worshipped as the Lord without beginning or end: Vishnu addresses him as the one “whose body is without a beginning or an end”; the column of fire has no root or top. Shiva Purana, Vidyeshvara Samhita 7.30; 9.19 (tr. J. L. Shastri)
  11. When the weapons Brahma and Vishnu hurled at each other began to burn the three worlds, he stood between them as a column of fire and both weapons fell into it; they set out to find its top and foot. Shiva Purana, Vidyeshvara Samhita 7.9–15
  12. He names that day Shivaratri. Shiva Purana, Vidyeshvara Samhita 9.8–10
  13. He burns the three cities with a single arrow. Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, Yuddha Khanda 10.25–28
