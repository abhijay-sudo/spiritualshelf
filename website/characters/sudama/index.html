Gods & Puranas

Sudama

सुदामा

Kuchela · Friend of Krishna

Krishna’s poor brahmana friend, deep in the Veda and master of his senses, is remembered for the flat rice he was too ashamed to offer. Tradition calls him Sudama, and the South, Kuchela. As Sandipani’s pupils, he and Krishna once wandered a storm-lashed forest all night, hand in hand. Urged by his starving wife, he carried the rice to Dvaraka in a rag. Krishna rose, wept as he held him, washed his feet, then drew out the bundle and ate a handful with joy. Sudama asked for nothing and came home to palaces. Rich at last, he longed to love and serve that friend, birth after birth.

Where this comes from

What this page about Sudama was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Unnamed, ‘a certain brahmana friend’ of Krishna (kṛṣṇasyāsīt sakhā kaścid brāhmaṇaḥ), learned, detached, peaceful, senses subdued: Bhagavata Purana 10.80.6 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  2. Lives on what comes unasked; poorly clothed (ku-caila); wife emaciated: 10.80.7 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  3. The name Sudama is not in the Sanskrit verses of 10.80-81; it is supplied in brackets by the Bhaktivedanta translation and by tradition. The name Kuchela in the South is tradition, not verified from a primary source. Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  4. Fellow pupil of Sandipani; the firewood errand and the storm, hand in hand: 10.80.27, 10.80.31, 10.80.35-39 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  5. Wife’s plea; four handfuls of flat rice tied in torn cloth: 10.80.8-14 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  6. Ashamed to offer the rice; Krishna eats one handful: 10.81.5, 10.81.8-10 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  7. Did not ask for anything: 10.81.14 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  8. Palaces at home; lives free of greed; reaches Krishna’s abode: 10.81.21-23, 10.81.38, 10.81.40 Sources of the story “Krishna and Sudama”
  9. Seeing him from a distance, Krishna rose at once, went forward and embraced him; at the touch of his dear friend the lotus-eyed Lord shed tears. Bhagavata Purana 10.80.18-19 (sahasotthāya … dorbhyāṁ paryagrahīt; vyamuñcad ab-bindūn netrābhyām)
  10. Krishna seated him on the couch and himself washed his feet, taking the water on his own head. Bhagavata Purana 10.80.20-22 (avanijyāsya pādau … agrahīc chirasā)
  11. Too embarrassed to offer the flat rice, he kept his head bowed; Krishna himself took the rice, tied in old cloth, from his garment. Bhagavata Purana 10.81.5 (vrīḍitaḥ … na prāyacchad avāṅ-mukhaḥ); 10.81.8 (svayaṁ jahāra)
  12. Krishna accepted the single palmful of flat rice with pleasure (his gift eaten ‘with joy’). Bhagavata Purana 10.81.35 (pṛthukaika-muṣṭiṁ pratyagrahīt prīti-yutaḥ); 10.81.10 (muṣṭiṁ sakṛj jagdhvā)
  13. He had always been poor and became rich only through Krishna’s glance (‘Rich at last’). Bhagavata Purana 10.81.33 (śaśvad daridrasya samṛddhi-hetuḥ … avalokataḥ)
  14. Seeing the riches, he wished for love, friendship, sympathy and service to Krishna birth after birth (‘longed to love and serve that friend, birth after birth’). Bhagavata Purana 10.81.36 (sauhṛda-sakhya-maitrī-dāsyaṁ punar janmani janmani syāt)
  15. The name Kuchela: the Narayaniyam calls him “the brahmana named Kuchela” who became Krishna’s fellow pupil in Sandipani’s house. That he is “Kuchela in the South” remains marked as tradition; the Bhagavata’s 10.80-81 only calls him ku-caila, “poorly dressed” (10.80.7). Narayaniyam of Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri, Dashaka 87.1 (kucēlanāmā bhavataḥ satīrthyatāṃ gataḥ sa sāndīpanimandirē dvijaḥ), Sanskrit text
