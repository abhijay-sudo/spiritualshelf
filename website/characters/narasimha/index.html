Narasimha

नृसिंह

Narahari

Narasimha, Vishnu as man and lion at once, came out of a pillar to make a child’s word true. Brahma’s boon barred Hiranyakashipu’s death by man or beast, indoors or out, on earth or in the sky, by any weapon; so the Lord came as neither, and ended him in a doorway, across his thighs, with his nails.

Names

Narasimha. Also known as: Narahari.

Where this comes from

What this page about Narasimha was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. The Bhagavata calls him Narahari (also Nrisimha and Nrihari). Bhagavata Purana 7.8.19 (nṛsiṁha-rūpaḥ), 7.8.27 (nṛharim)
  2. The Bhagavata calls him Narahari (also Nrisimha and Nrihari). Bhagavata Purana 7.10.25 (naraharim)
  3. Appeared from the pillar to make true the words of his servant; a form neither beast nor man. Bhagavata Purana 7.8.17–18 (satyaṁ vidhātuṁ nija-bhṛtya-bhāṣitam; na mṛgaṁ na mānuṣam)
  4. Terms of Brahma’s boon: not indoors or outdoors, by day or night, on the ground or in the sky, by weapon, man or beast. Bhagavata Purana 7.3.35–38
  5. Places Hiranyakashipu on his thighs in the doorway and tears him with his nails. Bhagavata Purana 7.8.29
  6. Brahma, Shiva and the gods cannot approach him in his anger; Lakshmi too holds back; Prahlada goes; the Lord places his hand on the boy’s head. Bhagavata Purana 7.9.1–5
  7. Tells Prahlada his father is purified, along with twenty-one forefathers. Bhagavata Purana 7.10.18
