Ramayana

Jatayu

जटायु

Gridhraraja

Jatayu, king of vultures and old friend of Dasharatha, is remembered for the fight he could not win.

Read more

Son of Aruna and brother of Sampati, he had offered to guard Sita at Panchavati; when Ravana carried her off he fought him, old as he was, until Ravana’s sword cut away his wings, and he lived just long enough to tell Rama who had taken her, and which way. Rama said the bird was as worthy of honour as Dasharatha, and gave him a kinsman’s last rites.

Names

Jatayu. Also known as: Gridhraraja.

Where this comes from

What this page about Jatayu was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Introduces himself as a friend of Rama’s father; son of Aruna, brother of Sampati; offers to guard Sita Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda, Sarga 14, verses 3 and 33–34
  2. Names himself king of vultures (gridhraraja), sixty thousand years old, and warns Ravana to release Sita Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda, Sarga 50, verses 3–5 and 20–28
  3. Breaks Ravana’s bows, armour and chariot and kills the charioteer; Ravana’s sword cuts off his wings Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda, Sarga 51, verses 9–19 and 42–43
  4. Dying, tells Rama that Ravana, son of Vishravas, took Sita southward Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda, Sarga 68, verses 9–17
  5. Rama: he is as worthy of honour to me as Dasharatha; cremates him and offers the rites as for his own kin Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda, Sarga 68, verses 26–36
