Ramayana

Sumitra

सुमित्रा

Saumitri-janani

Sumitra is one of King Dasharatha’s three queens. When the being from the fire brings the gods’ payasa, the king gives Kausalya half and Sumitra half of what is left; Kaikeyi has half of the remainder, and the last share, after a moment’s thought, goes to Sumitra again.

Read more

Under Ashlesha, the serpent’s star, with Cancer rising, she bears two sons, heroes skilled in every weapon, whom Vasishtha names Lakshmana and Shatrughna. Lakshmana grows up as Rama’s breath moving outside his body; Shatrughna is dearer than life to Bharata.

Names

Sumitra. Also known as: Saumitri-janani.

Where this comes from

What this page about Sumitra was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. One of Dasharatha’s three queens: 1.15.20; 1.16.27-29. Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  2. Given half of the half left after Kausalya’s share (a quarter); Kaikeyi given half of what then remained (an eighth); the remaining half (the last eighth) given, after the king had thought it over (anucintya), to Sumitra again: 1.16.27-29 (sumitrāyai… punar eva). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  3. Bears Lakshmana and Shatrughna, heroes skilled in all weapons (vīrau sarvāstrakuśalau): 1.18.14 (the IIT Kanpur text numbers it 1.18.13). The two sons of Sumitra (saumitrī) born under Sarpa, the serpent’s star (Ashlesha), in Cancer (kulīra), when the sun had risen: 1.18.15 (the IIT Kanpur text numbers it 1.18.14). Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  4. Vasishtha names Sumitra’s sons Lakshmana and Shatrughna after eleven days: 1.18.21-22. Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  5. From childhood on (bālyāt prabhṛti… nityaśaḥ, 1.18.28b-29a), Lakshmana as Rama’s other life-breath outside his body (bahiḥprāṇa ivāparaḥ): 1.18.28b-30a. Shatrughna dearer than life to Bharata: 1.18.32b-33a. Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
  6. The title “Saumitri-janani” (mother of the Saumitris) is a plain descriptive compound resting on 1.18.15, where the two brothers are called saumitrī, “the two sons of Sumitra”; not a quoted epithet. Sources of the story “The birth of Rama”
About these sources
  • Her later story is not told here and is not claimed.
