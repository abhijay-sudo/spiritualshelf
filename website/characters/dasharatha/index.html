Ramayana

Dasharatha

दशरथ

Kosaleshvara

Dasharatha of Ikshvaku’s line ruled Kosala from Ayodhya, a royal sage learned in the Veda and loved by town and country, who guarded his people as Manu did. He fought for Indra against the demon Shambara, and by sacrifice won four sons. Bound by two boons owed to Kaikeyi, he sent Rama into exile. On the sixth night he remembered an ascetic’s son he had once shot by mistake, and the father’s curse, and after midnight he died calling on Rama.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Dasharatha was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Learned in the Veda, far-sighted, loved by town and country, a great chariot-warrior of the Ikshvakus, a royal sage like the great sages; guarded the world as Manu did Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 6, verses 1–5
  2. Went with Kaikeyi to fight for Indra against the demon Shambara; wounded, saved by her; granted two boons Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 9, verses 11–17
  3. Performs the rite for sons Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 16, verse 9
  4. Four sons are born: Rama, Bharata, Lakshmana and Shatrughna Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda, Sarga 18, verses 8–16
  5. Kaikeyi claims the boons; he is held by his promise Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 11, verses 18–29
  6. Cries “Stop!” after the chariot Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 40, verse 46
  7. Called lord of Kosala (kosaleshvara); on the sixth night after Rama’s departure he recalls shooting, by sound alone, an ascetic’s son at the Sarayu Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 63, verses 3–25
  8. The bereaved father’s curse, that he too would die grieving for a son; he dies after midnight calling on Rama Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda, Sarga 64, verses 53–55 and 72–79
