Mahabharata

Bhishma

भीष्म

Pitamaha · Gangaputra

Bhishma, grandsire of the Kurus, is revered for a vow so terrible it became his name: Ganga’s son Devavrata gave up throne and marriage so his father might wed. Begged to break it, he said, “Truth I would never renounce.” He led the Kauravas ten days, then from his bed of arrows taught Yudhishthira a king’s duties and the thousand names of Vishnu, and left his body only when the sun turned north.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Bhishma was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Of the cursed Vasus, Dyu alone must live long on earth, virtuous, obedient to his father and without children; Ganga says this son is Dyu. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section XCIX
  2. Devavrata renounces the throne and vows brahmacharya; gods, Apsaras and Rishis rain flowers and say "This one is Bhishma (the terrible)"; Santanu grants the boon of death at will. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section C
  3. The "grandsire of the Kurus" protected the army for ten nights and now lies on a bed of arrows. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Bhishma Parva, Section XIII
  4. Fallen, he does not let his life depart, waiting for the sun's northern course. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Bhishma Parva, Section CXX
  5. On the bed of arrows Bhishma agrees to discourse on duty; Yudhishthira asks first about kingly duties. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Santi Parva (Book 12), Section LV–LVI
  6. After fifty-eight nights on the arrows, when the sun has begun its northward course, Bhishma gives up his life-breaths. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Anusasana Parva (Book 13), Section CLXVII–CLXVIII
  7. Begged by Satyavati to raise heirs, Bhishma: "I would renounce three worlds, the empire of heaven, anything that may be greater than that, but truth I would never renounce." Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CIII
  8. Asked by Yudhishthira, Bhishma teaches that one should hymn Vasudeva, the Lord of the universe, "by uttering His thousand names", praising Vishnu; he recites "these thousand names of Vasudeva" (the hymn tradition knows as the Vishnu Sahasranama). Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Anusasana Parva (Book 13), Section CXLIX
