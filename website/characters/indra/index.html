Mahabharata

Indra

इंद्र

Devaraja · Wielder of the Vajra

Indra, king of the gods, called Shakra and the thousand-eyed, wields the thunderbolt and is father of Arjuna. In these stories his throne is lost and won back: Vamana wins back the heaven Bali took; the Goddess slays Mahishasura, who had seized it. When Krishna holds up Govardhana against his storm, Indra’s pride bows. For the Pandavas’ sake he came as a brahmana to beg Karna’s armour, and at the end came in his own chariot to carry Yudhishthira to heaven.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Indra was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Kunti invokes "Sakra (the king of the gods)", who begets Arjuna. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXIII
  2. He is called Shakra, Purandara, "deity of a 1,000 eyes" and "lord of all the deities"; he comes on a car, filling the firmament and the earth with sound, to take Yudhishthira to heaven. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Mahaprasthanika Parva (Book 17), Section 3
  3. Indra, disguised as a brahmana, asks Karna for his natural armour and earrings and gives a dart in exchange; Karna addresses him as "the very lord of the celestials". Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Vana Parva, Section CCCVIII
  4. Indra, angry that his sacrifice was set aside, sends the clouds of dissolution on Vraja; Krishna holds up Govardhana for seven days; Indra's pride is brought down. Bhagavata Purana 10.25.1-2, 19, 23-24
  5. Bali, after the Vishvajit sacrifice, takes Indra's kingdom; the gods leave heaven. Bhagavata Purana 8.15
  6. Vamana returns Indra to heaven. Bhagavata Purana 8.23
  7. Mahisha conquers the gods and becomes "the Indra"; Indra gives the Goddess a thunderbolt drawn from his own thunderbolt and a bell from his elephant Airavata. Devi Mahatmya ch. 2 = Markandeya Purana, Canto 82 (tr. F. E. Pargiter, 1904)
  8. The Goddess strikes off Mahisha's head with her great sword; "all the hosts of the gods rose to the highest exultation". Devi Mahatmya ch. 3 = Markandeya Purana, Canto 83 (tr. F. E. Pargiter, 1904)
  9. Surya tells Karna that Sakra will come disguised as a Brahmana "for the benefit of the sons of Pandu". Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Vana Parva, Section 298 (printed "CCLXLVIII" in this edition)
