Mahabharata

Duryodhana

दुर्योधन

Suyodhana · Eldest of the Kauravas

Duryodhana, eldest of Dhritarashtra’s hundred sons, was a proud prince, Balarama’s pupil at the mace, and the friend who made Karna king of Anga when his birth was questioned. But the epic calls him a portion of Kali. Envy of the Pandavas led him to the dice, to Draupadi dragged into the hall, and to refusing them land a needle’s point would cover. Bhima’s mace broke his thighs; as he lay, still proud, flowers fell from the sky.

Where this comes from

The notes this page about Duryodhana was checked against: each point, and where it is found.

  1. Dhritarashtra begets a hundred sons upon Gandhari; Duryodhana is the eldest; evil omens attend his birth. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXV
  2. When Kripa asks Karna his lineage, Duryodhana installs Karna as king of Anga; Karna asks only for his friendship. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXXVIII
  3. "Duryodhana ... was born of a portion of Kali on earth." Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section LXVII
  4. He is also called Suyodhana; he and Bhima enter the arena mace in hand. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Adi Parva, Section CXXXVI
  5. After the dice he has Draupadi brought and dragged into the assembly. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Sabha Parva, Section LXVI
  6. He bares his thigh to Draupadi; Bhima vows to break it. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Sabha Parva, Section LXX
  7. Balarama arrives to watch the mace-duel of Bhima and Duryodhana, "both of whom were his disciples". Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Shalya Parva (Book 9), Section 34
  8. Bhima fulfils the vow to break Duryodhana's thighs; after the fallen king's speech ("I have ... governed the wide Earth ... Who is there so fortunate as myself!") a shower of fragrant flowers falls from the sky. Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Shalya Parva (Book 9), Section 58, 61
  9. Duryodhana to Krishna: "even that much of our land which may be covered by the point of a sharp needle shall not ... be given by us unto the Pandavas." Mahabharata (tr. K. M. Ganguli), Udyoga Parva, Section CXXVII
