  1. A scroll world

    The Fourteen Lokas

    The Vishnu Purana counts seven spheres, from the earth up to Satya-loka, and seven regions of Patala beneath the earth. Scroll, and descend through them in its names and its words.

    ↓ Scroll to travel

  2. 1 / 14

    Satya-loka

    The sphere of truth, the inhabitants of which never again know death.

  3. 2 / 14

    Tapo-loka

    The sphere of penance, inhabited by the deities called Vaibhrajas, who are unconsumable by fire.

  4. 3 / 14

    Jana-loka

    Here Sanandana and other pure-minded sons of Brahma reside.

  5. 4 / 14

    Mahar-loka

    The sphere of saints, above Dhruva. Its inhabitants dwell in it throughout a Kalpa, or day of Brahma.

  6. 5 / 14

    Swar-loka

    The heavenly sphere: the interval between the sun and Dhruva, the pole-star.

  7. 6 / 14

    Bhuvar-loka

    The atmospheric sphere: the region that extends from the earth to the sun, in which the Siddhas and other celestial beings move.

  8. 7 / 14

    Bhur-loka

    The sphere of the earth: wherever earthy substance exists, which may be traversed by the feet.

  9. Below the earth

    Its depth below the surface is said to be seventy thousand yojanas, each of the seven regions of Patala extending downwards ten thousand.

    The sage Narada, returning from those regions, said that there the rays of the sun diffuse light, and not heat, by day, and the moon shines by night for illumination, not for cold.

  10. 8 / 14

    Atala

    Named first among the seven regions of Patala. Its soil is white.

  11. 9 / 14

    Vitala

    Named second. Its soil is black.

  12. 10 / 14

    Nitala

    Named third. Its soil is purple.

  13. 11 / 14

    Gabhastimat

    Named fourth. Its soil is yellow.

  14. 12 / 14

    Mahatala

    Named fifth. Its soil is sandy.

  15. 13 / 14

    Sutala

    Named sixth. Its soil is stony.

  16. 14 / 14

    Patala

    Named seventh. Its soil is of gold.

  17. Sesha, called Ananta

    Below the seven Patalas is the form of Vishnu which is called Sesha. He has a thousand heads, and the thousand jewels in his crests give light to all the regions.

    Sesha bears the entire world, like a diadem, upon his head, and he is the foundation on which the seven Patalas rest.

Travel it again

How this was made

The names and every line are from the Vishnu Purana, Book 2, chapters 5 and 7, lightly shortened from H. H. Wilson’s English translation of 1840. The Purana describes the seven spheres from the earth upward, and it names the seven regions of Patala in one list without saying which lies above which. Travelling from Satya-loka downward, with the seven regions in the order of that list, is this page’s own arrangement. Seven spheres and seven regions of Patala make the fourteen; the Purana itself counts them as seven and seven. Wilson notes that other Puranas name the lower seven differently: the Bhagavata and the Padma have Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talatala, Mahatala, Rasatala and Patala. The colours of the seven lower regions follow the soils the text gives them: white, black, purple, yellow, sandy, stony and gold. The colours above the earth, the light and the drifting motes are this page’s own drawing, and are not from the text.
