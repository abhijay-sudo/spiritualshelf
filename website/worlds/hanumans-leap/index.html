  1. A scroll world

    Hanuman’s Leap

    From Mahendra mountain, across a hundred yojanas of ocean, to the shore of Lanka. Scroll, and travel it with him.

    ↓ Scroll to travel

  2. The leap from Mahendra

    Mahendra mountain shook. Hanuman stood on its slopes, the whole army of vanaras watching, and the strength he had half forgotten began to rise in him.

    He pressed the mountain with his hands and feet. Trees shed every flower they held. Springs burst from the rock.

  3. He crouched. He sprang.

    “Like an arrow loosed by Rama,” he told them, “I will go to Lanka. If Sita is not there, I will search the heavens.”

    Uprooted trees flew after him for a while, like kin following one who leaves on a long journey, then fell away into the salt sea.

  4. A mountain rises from the sea

    Far out over open water, something gold broke the surface. Mainaka rose from the ocean like the sun through cloud, its golden peaks blazing like a hundred suns, and stood in the sky’s path.

    He touched the mountain with his hand, smiled, and was gone into the sky.

  5. Smaller than a thumb

    Surasa, mother of serpents, rose from the waves, jaws open. “You are my food. Enter my mouth.”

    Hanuman grew ten yojanas; her mouth opened twenty. Up they went, until her jaws gaped eighty and he stood ninety. In a blink he shrank to the size of a thumb, darted in and out, and stood in the air.

  6. Lanka, gold against the sea

    At last, a green shore. Hanuman shrank to his natural size, so no rakshasa would wonder. From a peak he looked down: Lanka, gold against the sea.

How this was made

Every heading and line above is taken word for word from Spritual’s retelling “Across the sea, a ring”. The first painting, Hanuman grown vast on Mahendra, belongs to “Hanuman reaches for the sun”; the others belong to the crossing itself. No painting of Surasa exists on this site, so her contest of sizes is drawn as two plain measures, with only the four figures the story gives: ten, twenty, eighty, ninety.

The gauge in the margin: the sarga measures the whole crossing at a hundred yojanas and gives no distance for Mainaka or for Surasa, so they are marked in the order they come and carry no number.
