01/ 96RamayanaIn 12 stories

Rama

राम

He gave up a throne for the forest. Begged to return, he said: “The sea may cross its shore, but I will not break my father’s word.”

  • Fourteen years, two sandals
  • The golden vessel from the fire
  • The bow that broke the sky
  • and 9 more stories

Meet RamaBegin their story

  1. RamaRama. Dharma made flesh: deep as the ocean, steady as the Himalaya, forgiving as the earth, and true to every word. In 12 stories.
  2. SitaSita. Daughter of the Earth, whom Brahma called Lakshmi herself: unshaken through exile, captivity and fire. In 9 stories.
  3. LakshmanaLakshmana. Rama’s brother, his life-breath walking outside his body, who would not let him go into exile alone. In 9 stories.
  4. UrmilaUrmila. Sita’s younger sister and Lakshmana’s bride, who stayed in Ayodhya the fourteen years he spent in the forest. In 1 story.
  5. HanumanHanuman. Strength, wisdom, humility and devotion in one: the son of the Wind, who called himself Rama’s servant. In 7 stories.
  6. BharataBharata. The brother who was handed a kingdom and gave it back, ruling fourteen years in the name of Rama’s sandals. In 4 stories.
  7. ShatrughnaShatrughna. Sumitra’s son, who was to Bharata what Lakshmana was to Rama, and dearer to him than life. In 2 stories.
  8. DasharathaDasharatha. King of Kosala, a royal sage loved by his people, who died of grief for the son his own word had exiled. In 4 stories.
  9. KausalyaKausalya. Dasharatha’s queen, to whom he gave half the gods’ payasa, and the mother of Rama. In 1 story.
  10. SumitraSumitra. Dasharatha’s queen, who was twice given a share of the gods’ payasa, and bore Lakshmana and Shatrughna. In 1 story.
  11. SugrivaSugriva. Son of the Sun and king of the vanaras, whose friendship, sworn before fire, gave Rama an army. In 4 stories.
  12. ValiVali. Son of Indra and king of Kishkindha, so mighty that he once carried Ravana under his arm across the oceans. In 1 story.
  13. JambavanJambavan. Ancient king of the bears, born of Brahma, whose words woke Hanuman to his own strength. In 4 stories.
  14. VayuVayu. The Wind: the breath that moves in every body, and the father of Hanuman. In 1 story.
  15. VibhishanaVibhishana. Ravana’s righteous brother, who asked Brahma only that his mind never leave dharma, even in danger. In 4 stories.
  16. GuhaGuha. King of the Nishadas at Shringaverapura, the friend Rama held as dear as his own self. In 1 story.
  17. SumantraSumantra. Dasharatha’s charioteer and counsellor, of whom Rama said: the Ikshvakus have no truer friend. In 2 stories.
  18. ShabariShabari. The ascetic of Matanga’s forest, perfected in tapas, whose lifelong waiting ended the day Rama came. In 1 story.
  19. JatayuJatayu. King of vultures and old friend of Dasharatha, who gave his life trying to save Sita. In 2 stories.
  20. JanakaJanaka. King of Mithila, revered as a rajarshi, who found Sita in the earth and gave her hand to Rama. In 2 stories.
  21. VishvamitraVishvamitra. The king who became a brahmarshi by tapas alone; guru to Rama, and by tradition seer of the Gayatri. In 2 stories.
  22. ParashuramaParashurama. Rama of the Axe, Jamadagni’s son: the warrior-sage before whom the kings of the earth once fell. In 1 story.
  23. VasishthaVasishtha. Brahmarshi and guru of the Ikshvaku kings, who gave Rama his name and, at the end, his crown. In 3 stories.
  24. ValmikiValmiki. Revered as the Adi Kavi, the first poet: his grief became a shloka, and the shloka became the Ramayana. In 1 story.
  25. NaradaNarada. Devarshi Narada, sage of the gods and knower of the three worlds, who gave Valmiki the story of Rama. In 8 stories.
  26. MainakaMainaka. The golden mountain hidden beneath the sea, who rose to offer Hanuman rest in mid-flight. In 1 story.
  27. RavanaRavana. Lord of Lanka: a great scholar of the Veda and, tradition holds, among the greatest of Shiva’s devotees. In 5 stories.
  28. MeghanadaMeghanada. Ravana’s mighty son, the warrior Brahma himself named Indrajit: he who conquered Indra. In 3 stories.
  29. KaikeyiKaikeyi. The queen who once bore the wounded Dasharatha from battle, and whose two boons sent Rama to the forest. In 2 stories.
  30. MantharaManthara. Kaikeyi’s lifelong maid, called Kubja, whose counsel turned a queen’s joy into Rama’s exile. In 1 story.
  31. ShurpanakhaShurpanakha. Daughter of the sage Vishravas, sister of Ravana, whose desire and whose wound set the war of Lanka in motion. In 2 stories.
  32. ShantanuShantanu. Hailed by all kings as King of Kings: Ganga’s husband, Bhishma’s father, whose touch made the old young again. In 1 story.
  33. SatyavatiSatyavati. The fragrant ferry-maiden of the Yamuna who became queen of the Kurus, and mother of the sage Vyasa. In 1 story.
  34. BhishmaBhishma (Devavrata). Pitamaha, grandsire of the Kurus: son of Ganga, whose terrible vow of renunciation gave him his name. In 2 stories.
  35. DronaDrona. Dronacharya, teacher of the Kaurava and Pandava princes, master of the Vedas and of every weapon. In 2 stories.
  36. ArjunaArjuna. Krishna’s devotee and friend, peerless archer of the Pandavas, to whom the Bhagavad Gita was spoken. In 4 stories.
  37. ShikhandiShikhandi. Drupada’s son, a prince of Panchala, once a woman and later a man, against whom Bhishma would not raise his bow. In 1 story.
  38. EkalavyaEkalavya. The Nishada prince who made a clay image his guru, and gave his right thumb as the teacher’s fee. In 1 story.
  39. YudhishthiraYudhishthira. Dharmaraja, eldest of the Pandavas and son of the god Dharma, named at birth foremost of the virtuous. In 5 stories.
  40. DraupadiDraupadi. Panchali, risen from King Drupada’s sacrificial altar: queen of the five Pandavas, named first of all women. In 1 story.
  41. BhimaBhima. Second of the Pandavas, son of the Wind and brother of Hanuman, named at birth foremost of the strong. In 1 story.
  42. ViduraVidura. Dharma born among men: the wisest counsellor of the Kurus, at whose house Krishna chose to eat. In 1 story.
  43. KarnaKarna. Danavira, son of Surya and Kunti, born in armour: the archer-king of Anga who refused no one who asked. In 3 stories.
  44. AbhimanyuAbhimanyu. Son of Arjuna and Subhadra, nephew of Krishna: the young hero who broke into the wheel array alone. In 1 story.
  45. SavitriSavitri. Princess of the Madras, born of a goddess’s boon, whose wisdom and constancy won her husband back from Death. In 1 story.
  46. SatyavanSatyavan. Prince of the Shalvas, raised in a forest hermitage; named Satyavan for the truthfulness of his parents. In 1 story.
  47. YamaYama. Son of Vivasvat and lord of the departed, called Dharmaraja because he deals one law to all beings. In 2 stories.
  48. KuntiKunti. Pritha, sister of Krishna’s father, raised by King Kuntibhoja: mother of Karna and the three elder Pandavas. In 1 story.
  49. SuryaSurya. The Sun, thousand-rayed maker of day, called Vivasvat; father of Karna and of Yama. In 2 stories.
  50. DhritarashtraDhritarashtra. The blind king of the Kurus, son of Vyasa, mighty as ten thousand elephants, whose eye was wisdom. In 2 stories.
  51. SanjayaSanjaya. Minister of King Dhritarashtra, given divine sight by Vyasa: the voice in which the Bhagavad Gita is told. In 1 story.
  52. DharmaDharma. Righteousness itself, honoured as a god: father of Yudhishthira, whom he tests three times. In 1 story.
  53. IndraIndra. King of the gods and wielder of the thunderbolt, lord of heaven and father of Arjuna. In 9 stories.
  54. DuryodhanaDuryodhana. Eldest of Dhritarashtra’s hundred sons: a Kuru prince, master of the mace, the friend who made Karna a king. In 4 stories.
  55. JayadrathaJayadratha. King of the Sindhus and son-in-law of Dhritarashtra, whose severe penance won a boon from Lord Shiva. In 1 story.
  56. ShakuniShakuni. Son of Subala and brother of Queen Gandhari: a chariot-warrior of Gandhara whom none could match at dice. In 1 story.
  57. KritavarmaKritavarma. Hridika's son, who gave Duryodhana an akshauhini of troops when Krishna's own army had already gone to him. In 1 story.
  58. ShivaShiva. Lord of Uma and of all beings, who rose as a pillar of fire without end and bore the falling Ganga. In 11 stories.
  59. VishnuVishnu. Narayana, who sustains the worlds and descends age after age: as Narasimha, as Vamana, as Rama, as Krishna. In 12 stories.
  60. BrahmaBrahma. The creator of the worlds, grandfather of all that lives, who grants the fruit of every tapas. In 7 stories.
  61. DeviDevi. The eternal Mother of the world. Remembered, says the Devi Mahatmya, Durga takes away every creature’s fear. In 3 stories.
  62. LakshmiLakshmi. Shri, goddess of fortune and mother of the three worlds, who rose from the churned ocean and chose Vishnu. In 1 story.
  63. ParvatiParvati. Uma, daughter of Himavan, revered by every world, who won Shiva by fierce tapas and is Ganesha’s mother. In 5 stories.
  64. HimavanHimavan. Lord of the mountains, Mena’s husband and Parvati’s father, who gave his daughter to Shiva. In 1 story.
  65. KamaKama. The god of love, Rati’s husband, whom Indra trusted above his own thunderbolt. In 1 story.
  66. GaneshaGanesha. Son of Parvati and Shiva, lord of the ganas, remover of obstacles, worshipped before all the gods. In 3 stories.
  67. KartikeyaKartikeya. Six-faced son of Shiva, general of the gods. “Among commanders I am Skanda,” says the Gita. In 3 stories.
  68. GangaGanga. The river of heaven, purifier of the worlds. “Among rivers I am Jahnavi,” Krishna says in the Gita. In 2 stories.
  69. KrishnaKrishna. The Lord the Bhagavata calls Bhagavan himself: cowherd of Vraja, Arjuna’s charioteer and teacher. In 7 stories.
  70. VasudevaVasudeva. Krishna’s father, who kept his word even to Kamsa and carried the newborn Lord across the swollen Yamuna. In 1 story.
  71. DevakiDevaki. Krishna’s mother, who in Kamsa’s prison carried the Lord in her heart as the east carries the rising moon. In 1 story.
  72. NandaNanda. Great-hearted chief of the cowherds of Vraja, in whose house Krishna grew up as a son. In 3 stories.
  73. YashodaYashoda. Nanda’s wife, the cowherd woman of Gokula who took the Lord of all the worlds for her own small son. In 2 stories.
  74. BalaramaBalarama. Rohini’s son and Krishna’s elder brother, named Rama for the joy he gives and Bala for his strength. In 2 stories.
  75. RukminiRukmini. Princess of Vidarbha and Krishna’s queen, a portion of Shri herself, who chose him on hearing his praises. In 1 story.
  76. SudamaSudama. Krishna’s fellow pupil and friend: the poor brahmana who brought him flat rice and asked for nothing. In 1 story.
  77. DhruvaDhruva. The five-year-old prince whose unbending tapas won him the sight of Vishnu and the pole star. In 1 story.
  78. NachiketaNachiketa. Vajashravasa's son, the boy given to Death, who asked Death himself whether a man still is after he dies. In 1 story.
  79. SunitiSuniti. Queen of Uttanapada and mother of Dhruva, whose counsel set her son on the path to Vishnu. In 1 story.
  80. PrahladaPrahlada. The asura prince no weapon could harm. “Among Daityas I am Prahlada,” Krishna says in the Gita. In 3 stories.
  81. NarasimhaNarasimha. Vishnu as man-lion, who came out of a pillar to make his devotee’s word true. In 2 stories.
  82. VamanaVamana. Vishnu born to Aditi as a dwarf brahmachari, who measured the universe in two strides. In 1 story.
  83. BaliBali. Prahlada’s grandson and lord of the three worlds, who kept his word to Vishnu though it cost him everything. In 2 stories.
  84. ShukracharyaShukracharya. Sage of Bhrigu’s line, guru of the asuras. “Among seers I am Ushanas,” says Krishna in the Gita. In 1 story.
  85. BhagirathaBhagiratha. The royal sage of Ikshvaku’s line, forefather of Rama, whose penance brought Ganga down to earth. In 1 story.
  86. SurathaSuratha. The king of Chaitra’s line who ruled the whole earth and guarded his people like his own children. In 1 story.
  87. SamadhiSamadhi. The merchant the Goddess calls best of his kind: offered any boon, he asked only for knowledge. In 1 story.
  88. HiranyakashipuHiranyakashipu. Son of Kashyapa, king of the Daityas, whose tapas scorched the worlds and who conquered all three. In 2 stories.
  89. HolikaHolika. In later tradition and the festival of Holi, the king's sister whom fire could not burn. In 1 story.
  90. MahishasuraMahishasura. Lord of the asuras, who fought the gods for a hundred years, conquered them all and reigned as Indra. In 1 story.
  91. KamsaKamsa. The mighty king of the Bhojas, who so feared his sister’s eighth child that he saw the Lord in every breath. In 1 story.
  92. SurapadmanSurapadman. Son of Kashyapa and Maya, whose ten-thousand-year sacrifice won from Shiva a thousand and eight universes to rule. In 1 story.
  93. SatiSati. Daksha’s daughter and Shiva’s wife, who gave up the body born of her father rather than hear Shiva reviled. In 1 story.
  94. DakshaDaksha. Lord of the Prajapatis, Brahma’s son and Sati’s father, whose great sacrifice at Kanakhala left out Shiva. In 1 story.
  95. VirabhadraVirabhadra. The blazing leader of ganas who rose from Shiva’s matted hair to break Daksha’s sacrifice. In 1 story.
  96. NandiNandi. Shiva’s attendant and favourite, Shilada’s son, who answered Daksha’s curse on Shiva with a curse of his own. In 1 story.
