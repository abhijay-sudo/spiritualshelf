A fasting king drinks only water before his guest returns. The sage’s fury makes a demon of fire, and the Lord’s disc answers.

Ambarisha and Durvasa. Also known as: Ambarisha Durvasa katha, Sudarshana chakra story.

  • Hospitality

A king was sweeping a temple floor with his own hands.

He was Ambarisha, Nabhaga’s son. The whole earth with its seven islands was his, and wealth that had no end. He held it all as a man holds something seen in a dream.

His mind stayed at the Lord’s feet. His words told the Lord’s praise. His hands cleaned the Lord’s house. His ears were for the Lord’s stories, his eyes for the Lord’s image and temples, his feet for the road to holy places, his head for bowing. For himself he wanted nothing.

The Lord was pleased with him. He set his own disc, the Sudarshana chakra, terror of every enemy, to stand guard over his devotee.

Then came a month of Kartika.

Three nights the king had fasted. Now he went down into the Yamuna to bathe.

For one full year Ambarisha and his queen, his equal in character, had kept the vow of Ekadashi and Dvadashi. This was its end, in Kartika. At Madhuvana he worshipped Hari: the great bathing , then fine cloth, ornaments, fragrant garlands. He honoured the brahmanas and gave them sixty crore cows, young and gentle, horns sheathed in gold, hooves in silver, clothed in fine cloth, each with her calf. He fed the brahmanas first, the best food there was.

They were content. They gave him leave to break his fast.

At that very moment a guest stood before him. Durvasa himself, the mighty .

Ambarisha rose to meet him. He gave the a seat and every honour, came to his feet and asked him to eat.

Durvasa accepted gladly. First, the rites. He went down to the Yamuna, sank into her blessed water and fixed his mind on Brahman.

He was still there when only half a muhurta of Dvadashi was left.

The king, who knew , turned to the brahmanas.

“To slight a brahmana is a fault. Not to break the fast while Dvadashi lasts is a fault. I will end the vow with water alone. Brahmanas say that to drink water is to have eaten, and also not to have eaten.”

He drank. Then, his mind on the Lord, he waited.

His rites were done. Durvasa came up from the Yamuna’s bank, and the king welcomed him, hands folded.

By his own insight the knew what the king had done.

He was very hungry. His body shook. His brows knotted over a twisted face.

“See the cruelty of this man! Drunk on his fortune, no devotee of Vishnu, he thinks himself the Lord, and he has stepped across . You invited me as your guest. You gave me nothing, and you ate. I will show you what that earns.”

Blazing with rage, he tore a matted lock from his head. Out of it he made a kritya, a creature of fire, like the fire that ends the world.

It came for the king.

It came burning, a blade in its hand, and the earth shook under its feet.

Ambarisha looked straight at it. He did not move from where he stood.

The Lord had set his disc on guard beforehand. As fire takes an angry snake, the Sudarshana burned the kritya.

Then it turned on the one who had made it.

Durvasa saw his work undone and the disc coming, and ran for his life. He made for a cave of Meru, with the disc close behind him. He fled through the sky, across the earth, into caverns and seas, to the worlds of the guardians, to heaven.

Wherever he ran, the unbearable disc was there.

With no protector anywhere, Durvasa came at last to Brahma. “Creator, self-born one, save me from the fire of the Unconquered!”

Brahma answered: “When his play is over he will burn this universe, and my own seat with it, by the mere bending of a brow. I, Shiva, Daksha, Bhrigu, the lords of creatures and of gods: we carry his command on our bowed heads.”

Refused, scorched, the fled to Shiva on .

“Child,” said Shiva, “against him we have no power. Universes in their thousands rise and vanish in him. I, Sanat-kumara, Narada, Brahma, Kapila: none of us knows the reach of his maya. This weapon is past bearing even for us. Go to Hari. He will do you good.”

Durvasa came to Vaikuntha, where the Lord dwells with Shri.

Burned by the disc, he fell trembling at the Lord’s feet. “Infallible one, I have done wrong. Not knowing your greatness, I offended those dear to you. Shelter me. Even a soul in hell goes free when your name is spoken.”

The Lord said: “Brahmana, I am in my devotees’ power. I am, as it were, not free. They left homes, wives, sons, wealth, life itself to come to me. How could I leave them? They are my heart and I am theirs. They know nothing but me, and I know nothing but them.

“Power thrown at the good turns back on the thrower. Go to Nabhaga’s son. Ask his pardon. Then peace will come.”

The disc was still burning him. Durvasa came back to Ambarisha and, in his pain, clasped the king’s feet.

Ambarisha was ashamed that such a should touch his feet. Pity hurt him sorely. He turned to the Lord’s weapon and prayed.

“Sudarshana, you are fire, sun and moon. You are water, earth, sky and wind. Thousand-spoked one, beloved of Achyuta, I bow to you. Let it be well with this brahmana. If our house has given gifts, offered sacrifice and kept its , if it has honoured brahmanas, let him be free of this burning. If the one Lord is pleased with us, let him be free.”

The disc grew calm. The burning stopped.

The king had still not eaten.

Freed from the fire, Durvasa praised the king and blessed him.

“Today I have seen the greatness of the Lord’s own people. I wronged you, and you sought my good. You overlooked my offence and saved my life.”

From the hour the fled to the hour he returned, a whole year had passed. In all that year Ambarisha, longing to see his guest again, had eaten nothing. He had lived on water.

Now he clasped Durvasa’s feet and fed him every good thing. The ate. “Now you eat,” he said with respect. “The women of heaven will sing of you, and so will this earth.”

He took his leave and rose through the sky toward Brahma’s world, praising the king.

Then Ambarisha ate.
