A boy asks the king of three worlds for three paces of land. His guru says: refuse. The king gives anyway.

Vamana and King Bali. Also known as: Vamana Avatar.

  • Truth
  • Generosity

Bali had fallen in battle with the gods. His guru Shukracharya, of Bhrigu’s line, brought him back to life, and Bali served him with all he had.

The Bhrigus set him to the Vishvajit sacrifice. Ghee went into the fire, and out of the fire came a chariot wrapped in gold and silk, yellow horses, a lion banner, a gilded bow, two quivers that never emptied, and armour. His grandfather Prahlada gave him a garland whose flowers never wilt. Shukracharya gave him a conch.

Bali blew it outside Indra’s golden gates.

“He looks ready to drink the universe,” Indra told his guru Brihaspati.

“Only the Lord himself can conquer him now. Leave heaven. Go unseen. Wait.”

The gods scattered. Bali took the three worlds.

Aditi, mother of the gods, grieved as if she had no one left.

After many days the Kashyapa rose from meditation and came home to find no joy there. “Your face is withered,” he said. “What has happened?”

“The demons have taken everything,” said Aditi. “Our wealth, our beauty, our fame, our home. We are exiles, sinking in a sea of sorrow.”

Kashyapa smiled. Then he taught her the vow Brahma had taught him: in the bright fortnight of Phalguna, for twelve days, live on milk alone and worship the lotus-eyed Lord.

Aditi kept it to the letter, reining in her mind like wild horses.

The Lord stood before her, in yellow silk, conch, disc, club and lotus in his four hands.

Aditi fell to the ground like a staff. Then she stood, hands folded, trembling, unable to speak through her tears.

“I know what you want,” the Lord said. “Your sons home, their enemies beaten. But the asura chiefs are guarded by brahmanas now. Force against them would bring no joy. Still, what is offered to me is never wasted. I will be born as your son. Tell no one. A secret succeeds when it is kept.”

On the twelfth day of the bright moon of Bhadra, with the sun at the meridian, he came from Aditi’s womb, four-armed, blazing. Kashyapa cried, “Jaya! Jaya!”

Then, before his father and mother, like an actor changing costume, the Lord became a dwarf, a boy brahmachari.

Vamana.

At his thread ceremony the gods brought gifts. The sun himself spoke the Gayatri to him. Brihaspati gave the sacred thread, the earth a deerskin, the moon a staff, Brahma a waterpot, Kubera a begging bowl. Bhagavati, Shiva’s wife, gave him his first alms.

Then Vamana heard that Bali was performing horse sacrifices, and set out. With every small step, the earth sank under his weight.

At Bhrigukaccha, on the north bank of the Narmada, the priests saw a sun rising close by. Their own splendour faded. Was it the sun god? Sanat-kumara? Agni, come to watch?

While they argued, a boy with matted locks, a staff, an umbrella and a waterpot walked into the arena.

The whole assembly rose. Bali, overjoyed, seated the boy, washed his feet and touched that water to his own head.

“Ask, and take,” he said. “A cow, gold, a house, villages, horses, elephants, chariots, a bride.”

“No one in your line has refused a brahmana,” said Vamana, “or broken a promise. I ask three paces of land, measured by my own steps.”

“You talk like an elder,” said Bali, “but you are a boy. I own the three worlds. I could give you a whole island.”

“A man not content with three paces,” said Vamana, “would not be content with an island. He would want the others too.”

Bali smiled. “As you like. Take it.” He lifted the waterpot to seal the gift.

Shukracharya saw what the boy was, and spoke at once.

“Son of Virochana, this dwarf is Vishnu, the imperishable, born to Aditi for the gods. He will take your land, your wealth, your fame, and hand them to Indra. One step will cover the earth. The second, the sky. His body will fill everything. Where will you put the third? You cannot keep this promise.”

Bali was silent a while. Then he answered.

“I am Prahlada’s grandson. Shall I take back my word like a common cheat? The earth once said no burden weighs on her like a liar. Hell, poverty, even death frighten me less than deceiving a brahmana.”

Shukracharya cursed him. “You will soon lose all your fortune.”

Bali did not waver.

Queen Vindhyavali, pearls at her throat, brought a golden waterpot. Bali washed the dwarf’s feet and poured out the gift.

Flowers fell from the sky. Drums thundered. “He knew whose side Vishnu was on,” the heavens sang, “and gave anyway!”

Then the dwarf began to grow.

He grew until earth, sky, seas, beasts, gods and were inside him. From Indra’s seat Bali saw the underworlds in the soles of his feet, mountains on his calves, stars across his chest, the sun in his eyes. Sudarshana burned in his hand.

One step covered the earth. His body filled the sky; his arms, every direction. The second step took heaven, the foot rising on, beyond Brahma’s world.

No place was left for a third.

Against Bali’s wishes, his asuras seized their tridents. “This is no brahmana! It is Vishnu!” Vishnu’s companions turned them back. “Stop,” Bali told his captains. “Time is against us.” At his word, they withdrew to the lower worlds.

When the sacrifice ended, Garuda bound Bali with the ropes of Varuna. A cry went through every world.

“You promised three steps,” said Vamana. “Two have taken the universe. Where shall I put the third?”

Bali had lost his radiance, but not his resolve. “My word will not be false. Place your third step on my head. I fear chains and hell less than dishonour.”

Then, as the moon comes up in the dark, Prahlada came. Bound, Bali could only bow his head, eyes full of tears.

Prahlada fell before the Lord. “You gave him the throne of heaven, and today you took it. Both were beautiful.”

“He has given you everything, even his body,” said Brahma. “Release him.”

“Stripped of his riches, bound, deserted by his kin, cursed by his guru,” said the Lord, “he would not give up the truth. So I give him what even the gods cannot reach. In the age of Manu Savarni he will be Indra. Until then he lives in Sutala, built by Vishvakarma at my order, where no one can defeat him. Bali, I will always be with you.”

Bali, weeping, bowed. The ropes of Varuna fell away. With Prahlada beside him, he went down into Sutala.

And Vamana gave heaven back to Indra.
