In Holika's lap

In the Bhagavata, fire cannot harm the boy. At Holi, a bonfire remembers the aunt who sat in the flames with him in her lap.

Holika Dahan. Also known as: Prahlad and Holika, the Holi bonfire.

  • Fearlessness
  • Devotion

Bathed and adorned by his mother, Prahlada was brought in and bowed at his father's feet. Hiranyakashipu gathered him into his arms, set him on his lap and breathed in the scent of his hair. His tears fell on the child's smiling face.

"Long may you live, my son. Tell me the best of what you have learned."

"To hear of Vishnu," said Prahlada, "to sing of him, remember him, serve and worship him, pray to him, be his servant and friend, and give him everything."

The king's lips trembled. He turned on the teacher Shanda. "You taught him this!"

"Not I, king, nor anyone. It grew in him."

The boy spoke again. Hiranyakashipu flung him from his lap. "Take him away. Kill him."

Coppery-bearded and sharp-toothed, the king's servants came with tridents, shouting, "Cut him! Pierce him!" Prahlada sat silent, his mind on the Lord. The blows did nothing.

Afraid now, the king tried other ways. The feet of great elephants. Huge serpents. Spells. A fall from a hilltop. Illusions. A prison. Poison. Hunger. Cold, and wind, and fire, and water. Heavy stones.

None of it could kill his son.

He fears nothing, thought the king. His strength is endless. My enmity with him will be my death. Or perhaps not. So thinking, Hiranyakashipu stayed silent, his face to the ground, his glow somewhat dimmed.

Fire was on that list. One word, between wind and water. The Bhagavata says no more.

Night falls on the eve of Holi, and a bonfire is lit. It has a name: Holika Dahan, the burning of Holika.

The story told of that fire is not in the Bhagavata. It belongs to later tradition and to the festival itself, and its tellings do not all agree. Many stories have gathered around Holi. This is one of the three told most often.

Here the quarrel is simple. The king wanted everyone in his kingdom to worship him. His own son, devoted to Vishnu, would not. Hiranyakashipu tried again and again to kill the boy, and nothing he did could touch him.

Then the king's sister stepped into the story.

Her name was Holika, and fire could not burn her.

Why? Here the tellings part ways. Some say she had received a boon that kept the flames from her. Others say it was something she wore, a scarf or a cloak that fire would not touch.

Whose plan was it? They part again. In some, the king asked his sister for help. In others, she offered it herself.

Either way, the plan was fire. A pyre would burn. Holika would sit in it with Prahlada in her lap. Her protection would hold. The boy, with nothing to protect him, would not come out.

The pyre burned. Holika sat in it, the boy in her lap.

It did not end as planned. Holika burned to death. Prahlada came out of the fire unharmed.

So the story goes. The tellings differ on how it happened. In one, the boy kept chanting the name of Vishnu and came through unscathed. In another, her protection left her and passed to him, for he was devoted to Vishnu. In another, as the fire rose, the cloth flew off Holika and covered the boy instead.

The king's sister was gone. His son, once again, was alive.

That burning is what the bonfire remembers. It is lit on the night before Holi, in North India and Nepal, in Maharashtra, Assam and beyond. The night has another name too: Chhoti Holi. Customs change from place to place. People walk around the fire. Into it go coconuts, barley, wheat and cakes of cow dung. In the south the same night keeps another fire and another story, Kama Dahanam, when Shiva burned Kama, the god of love. Then morning comes. It is Holi, and the colours fly.

The Bhagavata carries Prahlada's story on to his father's assembly hall, where the king asks whether the Lord is in this pillar, strikes it with his fist, and Narasimha comes out of it.

(12)

Original retellings, not scripture text or translations.

  1. Bhagavata Purana 7.5.19-26, 7.5.28-34, 7.5.39-48 (Prahlada on his father's lap; his answer; the teacher's reply; the king's second question and the boy's second answer, compressed; thrown from the lap; the tridents; the attempts on his life, fire among them; the king's despair)
  2. Bhagavata Purana 7.8.12-18 (the king asks if the Lord is in the pillar, strikes it, and Narasimha comes out of it)
  3. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Holi", section "Myths and stories associated with Holi" (Holi's trademark smearing of colours, and its colour-throwing festivities; many myths, the three told most often among them Hiranyakashipu and Prahlada)
  4. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Holi", section "Hiranyakashipu and Prahlada" (the king who wanted to be worshipped; his sister Holika, immune to fire, with a protective scarf in some variants; the pyre; her protection passing to Prahlada; Holika Dahan on Choti Holi, the day before Holi)
  5. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Holi", section "Shiva and Kama" (Shiva burns Kama, the god of love)
  6. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Holi", section "Regional variations" (the Holika bonfire in North India; Kama Dahanam and the story of Shiva and Kama in the south)
  7. Wikipedia, "Holika", section "Legend" (the king's various attempts to kill Prahlada; his sister Holika offers her help; a boon, or in a variant a scarf or fireproof garment; Prahlada on her lap atop the burning pyre; the name of Vishnu; the garment flying off her and covering him)
  8. Wikipedia, "Holika", section "Holika Dahan" (walking around the fire; coconuts, barley, wheat and cow dung offered into it; Kama Dahanam on the same date in South India)
  9. Wikipedia, "Holika Dahana", lead and section "Significance" (Prahlada on Holika's lap in the bonfire; pyres burnt the night before Holi in North India, Maharashtra, Assam, Nepal and elsewhere)
  10. Wikipedia, "Holi", section "Background" (the king demanded that everyone worship him; cruel punishments, none of which affected the boy; Holika's cloak that fire could not harm, which flew from her and encased Prahlada as the fire spread). This section also says the legend is found in "the 7th chapter of the Bhagavata Purana"; the Bhagavata's Prahlada chapters (7.4-7.10) do not name Holika.
  11. Wikipedia, "Holi", section "Holika Dahan" (the night before Holi is called Holika Dahan or Chhoti Holi; people gather around the bonfire)
  12. Wikipedia, "Holi", section "Bihar, Eastern Uttar Pradesh and Jharkhand" (dried cow dung cakes put into the Holika bonfire)
