Out of a dust-dark whirlwind, the axe-bearing son of Jamadagni bars the road home and dares young Rama to draw the bow of Vishnu.

Parashurama meets Rama. Also known as: Parshuram and Ram.

The road home from . When the night had passed, Vishvamitra had taken leave of the two kings, of Rama and of Lakshmana, and gone north to the Himalaya. Janaka had given his daughters countless cows, silks, elephants, horses, chariots and gold, and turned back. Now Dasharatha rode for with his four sons, the ahead, the army behind.

Then the birds began to shriek on every side. Yet the beasts of the earth passed around him on the auspicious side.

“My heart sinks at the birds,” the king said to Vasishtha, “and rises at the beasts. Why?”

“The birds foretell a terror sent by fate,” said Vasishtha. “The beasts say it will pass. Let fear go.”

As he spoke, a fierce wind rose.

It came as a whirlwind, shaking the earth, wrenching at great trees. Darkness swallowed the sun. No one could tell one quarter from another. Dust wrapped the army until it stood senseless.

Only Vasishtha and the , the king and his sons kept their wits.

And in that darkness they saw him.

The son of Jamadagni. Bhargava Rama, Parashurama, who had humbled the kings of kings. Matted hair wild about his head. Unshakable as , unbearable as the fire at the end of an age, blazing so bright that common eyes could not hold him. An axe hung from his shoulder. In his hand a bow like massed lightning, and a fierce arrow ready to strike.

He looked like Shiva, destroyer of the three cities.

The who tend the sacred fires drew together and whispered.

“Has he come to root out the kshatriyas again? His father’s murder drove him to it once. But that rage was spent. Will he begin it all once more?”

Still, they went to him with water of welcome. “Rama,” they said gently. “Rama.”

He accepted Vasishtha’s honour. Then he turned to the other Rama, Dasharatha’s son.

“Your valour is talked of everywhere. I have heard you broke Shiva’s bow. Hearing it, I came, and I have brought another bow. This terrible bow came down to me through Jamadagni. Fit an arrow and draw it to your ear. Show me your strength. If you are worthy, I will give you a duel.”

Dasharatha’s face fell. He joined his palms.

“You are a brahmana of great austerity. Your anger against the kshatriyas was quieted long ago. Grant my sons safety; they are only boys.

“You are born of Bhrigu’s line, devoted to the Veda and to vows. You promised thousand-eyed Indra that you had laid down weapons. You gave the whole earth to Kashyapa and went to the forest, to Mount Mahendra.

“Or have you come to destroy us all? If Rama alone is slain, none of us will live.”

Bhargava did not heed him. The father’s pleading fell away unheard. He spoke to Rama, and only to Rama.

“There were two bows,” he said.

“Vishvakarma made them: unsurpassed, mighty, worshipped by every world. The gods gave one to Shiva for his war on Tripura. That bow destroyed Tripura, Kakutstha, and that is the bow you broke. The other they gave to Vishnu. It is this one.

“Once the gods asked Brahma which was stronger, Shiva or Vishnu. Brahma, wanting the truth shown, set the two against each other. A fierce war followed. Then Vishnu gave a single hum. Shiva’s mighty bow went slack, and the three-eyed god stood frozen.

“Gods, and charanas begged them for peace, and peace came. But from that day the gods held Vishnu the greater. And Shiva, in anger, gave his bow, arrow still upon it, to Devarata, king of Videha.

“This bow Vishnu gave in trust to Richika of Bhrigu’s line. Richika gave it to his son, my father Jamadagni.

“My father had laid all weapons aside. Kartavirya Arjuna, savage of mind, put him to death. When I heard of that foul killing, I rooted out the kshatriyas as fast as they were born, not once but many times. I won the whole earth, performed a sacrifice, and at its end gave the earth to Kashyapa. Now I dwell on Mahendra, and the power of my tapas is in me.

“I heard of the broken bow and came at once. Take this bow of my fathers. Remember you are a kshatriya. Fit the arrow. Take aim. If you can, then we fight.”

Rama answered with courtesy, for his father stood near.

“Bhargava, I have heard what you did to free yourself from your debt to your father, and we honour it. But you belittle me, as if I lacked a kshatriya’s strength. Now see my power.”

Swift-handed, in anger, he took the bow of Vishnu and its arrow from Bhargava’s hand. He strung the bow, set the arrow, and took aim.

“You are a brahmana, and worthy of my reverence, and bound to Vishvamitra. I will not loose this at your life. But the arrow of Vishnu never falls in vain. Your swift motion, or the worlds your tapas won. Choose.”

Above them gathered Brahma and the gods, gandharvas, apsaras, siddhas, kinnaras, yakshas and nagas, to watch.

As Rama took aim, the worlds went senseless. The strength drained out of Jamadagni’s son. He stared up at the lotus-eyed prince, and spoke slowly, softly.

“When I gave the earth to Kashyapa, he told me: do not live in my domain. Since then I never spend a night on earth. Do not take my motion, Raghava. I will go to Mahendra swift as thought. But the peerless worlds I won by tapas: strike those. Do not delay.

“By your touch on this bow I know you. You are the undying Lord of gods, slayer of Madhu. All the gods are watching you. It is no shame to be faced down by the lord of the three worlds. Loose the arrow.”

Rama loosed it.

The worlds of Bhargava’s tapas were gone. The darkness lifted from every quarter, and gods and praised Rama. Jamadagni’s son praised him too, circled him in reverence, and went his way to Mahendra.

Rama, calm again, placed the bow of Vishnu in the hands of Varuna. He bowed to Vasishtha, then turned to his shaken father. “Bhargava has gone. Order the army on to .”

Dasharatha held his son and kissed his head. He felt his son and himself born again.

Word meanings & sources

Tap any underlined word for its meaning, what it means in this story, and where it comes from.

  1. Mithila मिथिला mithilā

    Literal“City of Mithila.” Named for King Mithila, said to be born from the churning (mathana) of his father Nimi’s body (Bhagavata 9.13.13). Its kings are also called Vaideha and Janaka.

    MeaningJanaka’s kingdom, where Sita grew up and Shiva’s bow was kept.

  2. Ayodhya अयोध्या ayodhyā

    Literal“Not to be fought against”; a + yudh.

    MeaningRama’s city, on the river Sarayu.

  3. sage ऋषि ṛṣi

    Literal“Seer.” One who perceives; the Vedic word for a poet-visionary who hears rather than invents.

    MeaningA rishi: a seer, teacher or ascetic living in the forest.

  4. Kailasa कैलास kailāsa

    MeaningThe Himalayan mountain that is Shiva’s home.
