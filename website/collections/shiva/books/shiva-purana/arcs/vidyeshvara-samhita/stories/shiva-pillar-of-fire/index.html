The fire with no end

Brahma and Vishnu go to war over who is lord. A pillar of fire rises between them. One seeks its foot, one its top. One comes back with a lie.

Lingodbhava. Also known as: the pillar of fire, Brahma, Vishnu and the endless linga.

Vishnu slept on his serpent couch, in splendour, his attendants around him.

Brahma, foremost of those who know the Veda, came that way.

"Who are you, lying there so proudly though you see me? Get up, child. Look: your lord has come. One who slights an elder owes a penance."

Vishnu was angry, but outwardly calm. "Welcome, child. Sit. Why is your face so fierce?"

"I am the Grandfather of the world," said Brahma, "and your protector too."

"The world rests in me," said Vishnu, "and you reason like a thief. You were born from the lotus of my navel. You are my son."

"I am the greater, not you!"

"I am the lord!"

Each meant to destroy the other. They made ready for war.

Brahma rode the swan. Vishnu rode Garuda. Behind them their followers crashed together.

The gods came in their sky-chariots to see so strange a war, and scattered flowers over it from above.

Vishnu, furious, loosed unbearable arrows at Brahma's chest, and weapon after weapon. Brahma answered with arrows like fire. The watching gods cried out, shaken.

Then Vishnu, breathing hard, aimed the Maheshvara weapon at Brahma. Brahma, in a rage that shook the universe, aimed the dreadful Pashupata at Vishnu's chest. It climbed the sky bright as ten thousand suns, thousand-mouthed, with a roar like a storm wind.

The two weapons faced each other.

The gods turned to one another in dread, like subjects whose kings have fallen out.

Afraid, the gods held one thought: creation, preservation, dissolution, concealment and grace all flow from the bearer of the trident. Without his grace no blade of grass is destroyed.

They went to 's peak, bowed before his dwelling, shaped like the syllable Om, and entered.

Shiva sat with Uma on a jewelled seat among his ganas. The gods, eyes brimming, fell flat like staffs, far off. He called them near.

"Children, is all well? I know of this war already. Your distress only tells it again." He smiled, as one soothes children.

He summoned a hundred gana chiefs and mounted an Om-shaped chariot, the Goddess beside him. Music played. Indra and the gods followed.

Above the battlefield he hid in the clouds. The music stopped.

Below, the weapons' flames were burning the three worlds: an ending before its time.

The formless one stood between them as a pillar of fire, vast, terrible. Both weapons, each able to destroy the worlds, fell into it and were stilled.

"What is this wonder?" they asked. "A pillar of fire, beyond the senses! We must find its top and its foot."

Proud of their strength, they set off at once.

"Together we will get nothing done," said Vishnu. He became a boar and went down to find the root. Brahma became a swan and rose to see the summit.

The boar broke through the netherworld and went far beyond. He saw no foot. Exhausted, he returned to the battlefield.

Brahma was still climbing.

High in the sky Brahma saw a wonder: a ketaki flower, falling.

It had fallen for many years and was still fragrant, still unfaded. Shiva, watching the two, had laughed; his head shook, and the flower dropped, to bless them both.

"King of flowers," said Brahma, "who wore you? Why do you fall?"

"I have been falling from the middle of this pillar, which no one can measure, for a very long time. Give up hope of seeing its end."

"Friend, now do as I wish. Stand with me before Vishnu and say: 'Brahma has seen the top of the pillar. I am the witness.'"

He bowed to the flower, again and again. In distress, the saying goes, even an untruth is allowed.

Vishnu stood on the battlefield, spent and joyless. Brahma, seeing him, danced for joy.

Vishnu told the truth: he had found no foot.

"Hari, I have seen its top," said Brahma. "This ketaki is my witness."

"It is so," said the flower.

Vishnu believed it, bowed, and worshipped him with sixteen rites.

To punish the deceit, the Lord stepped from the fire in visible form. Vishnu rose, hands trembling, and clasped his feet. "You have no beginning, no end. Deluded, we went searching. Forgive us. This too was your play."

"Child, I am pleased," said Shiva. "You wished to be lord, yet spoke the truth. You shall be honoured as I am, with your own temples, festivals and worship."

Brahma was not yet answered.

From between his brows Mahadeva brought forth a wondrous being, Bhairava, to break Brahma's pride.

Bhairava bowed. "What is my task, Lord? Command me quickly."

"Child, here is Brahma, first deity of the worlds. Worship him: with your sharp, swift sword."

Bhairava seized the hair of Brahma's fifth head, the proud one that had lied, and raised his sword to cut.

Brahma shook like a plantain in a gale. Ornaments scattered, garland and robes awry, he fell at Bhairava's feet.

Then Vishnu wept. His tears wet Shiva's feet; hands folded, he pleaded like a child with its father.

"It was you who gave him his five faces, long ago. Forgive this first offence. Show him grace."

Before all the gods, Shiva called Bhairava off.

Brahma stood with bowed neck.

"Hungry for honour, you played the lord by deceit," said Shiva. "You shall have none: no shrine, no festival."

"Lord, that my head is spared I count a boon."

"Child, a world that fears no king does not last. Punish the guilty; bear the world's yoke. And a rare boon: you shall be guru of every sacrifice. Without you, none bears fruit."

To the false witness: "Ketaki, deceiver, go far from here. I want you in my worship no more."

"Then my birth is fruitless," said the flower. "Forgive me. I have seen you: can a lie stain me?"

"I cannot wear you; my word stands. But my people shall, and as a canopy you will hang above me."

Brahma and Vishnu stood silent at Shiva's right and left, hands folded.

They seated him with his family on a splendid seat and worshipped him: necklaces, crowns, silk, garlands, lamps, a white umbrella. Delighted, he gave it all away.

"Children, your worship this great day pleases me. This day shall be known as Shivaratri. This pillar without beginning or end will become small, so the worlds may see and worship it. It rose like a mountain of fire: it shall be famed as Arunachala."

With a stream of nectar he raised the armies fallen in battle.

"Give up your pride. This pillar is my linga. Worship it always."

He laid his hand on their heads and taught them Om.

Where he stood, he was gone.
