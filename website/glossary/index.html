Glossary

Words the stories use, in plain meaning. In a story, tap any underlined word to see the same note.

ashrama आश्रम āśrama

A hermitage: the dwelling of a sage and those who study with them.

Literally: “A place of exertion.” From śram, to strive; a hermitage where effort is quiet and daily.

In Sita Swayamvar: Valmiki’s ashrama is where the Ramayana’s first question is asked. The forest setting matters: the question comes from stillness, not from a court.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 1.1

avatar

A descent of a deity into the world in living form.

Ayodhya अयोध्या ayodhyā

Rama’s city, on the river Sarayu.

Literally: “Not to be fought against”; a + yudh. The unconquerable city on the Sarayu.

In Sita Swayamvar: Rama’s home. Janaka sends word there when the bow breaks.

dharma

What upholds: duty, order and right action, as the situation asks.

dharma-sankata धर्म-संकट dharma-saṅkaṭa

A crisis where two duties pull in opposite directions and no choice is clean.

Literally: Dharma, what holds; saṅkaṭa, a narrow pass. A tight place between obligations.

The hinge of every great dilemma in the Itihasa: Rama at the moment of exile, Yudhishthira at the gates.

exile वनवास vanavāsa

“Dwelling in the forest.” Rama’s fourteen years away from Ayodhya.

Literally: Vana, forest; vāsa, dwelling. Not a punishment word: a word for where one lives.

In Sita Swayamvar: Narada names the exile as one chapter of a whole life, beside friendships and return.

extraordinary weapons अस्त्र astra

Missile-weapons released by mantra rather than by hand; knowledge, not metal.

Literally: From as, to throw. Distinct from śastra, a held blade; an astra is knowledge that is invoked.

In Sita Swayamvar: Vishvamitra gives the brothers astras in the forest. The story is careful to say the knowledge is not for display.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana

furrow सीता sītā

The line a plough cuts in the earth. Sita’s name means exactly this.

Literally: Sītā: “furrow.” An old Vedic word for the ploughed line, honoured as a goddess of the field.

In Sita Swayamvar: Her name is the memory of where she was found. Before any title, she is the earth’s daughter.

Itihasa

“Thus it was.” The Ramayana and Mahabharata, told as remembered history.

Kailasa

The Himalayan mountain that is Shiva’s home.

Kanda

A book of the Ramayana. There are seven.

Kishkindha

The vanara kingdom ruled by Vali and later by Sugriva. Rama first meets Hanuman and the exiled Sugriva outside the city, near Mount Rishyamuka and Lake Pampa.

maryada मर्यादा maryādā

The boundary one keeps; conduct that stays within what is fitting.

Literally: A shoreline, a limit. Rama is called Maryada Purushottama: the best of men within limits.

In Sita Swayamvar: Why Rama waits for permission before touching the bow.

Mithila मिथिला mithilā

Janaka’s kingdom, where Sita grew up and Shiva’s bow was kept.

Literally: “City of Mithila.” Named for King Mithila, said to be born from the churning (mathana) of his father Nimi’s body (Bhagavata 9.13.13). Its kings are also called Vaideha and Janaka.

In Sita Swayamvar: Where Sita grew up and where the bow is kept. The marriage joins Mithila to Ayodhya.

nishkama karma निष्काम कर्म niṣkāma karma

Action done fully, without clinging to its fruit.

Literally: Niṣ-kāma, without desire; karma, action. Not inaction: action freed of bargaining.

Krishna’s teaching on the field of Kurukshetra, and the quiet way Rama handles the astras.

Source: Bhagavad Gita 2.47

Panchavati

The forest grove on the Godavari where Rama, Sita and Lakshmana built a hermitage.

Parva

A book of the Mahabharata. There are eighteen.

Purana

“Ancient.” A body of texts holding the stories of the gods, creation and kings.

rite यज्ञ yajña

A sacred offering made with fire and mantra; also any act performed as an offering.

Literally: From yaj, to worship, to offer. The fire-rite of the Vedas; later, any disciplined act given away.

In Sita Swayamvar: Janaka was ploughing the ground for a rite when he found Sita. The tradition reads her arrival as the earth’s reply to an offering.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana

sage ऋषि ṛṣi

A rishi: a seer, teacher or ascetic living in the forest.

Literally: “Seer.” One who perceives; the Vedic word for a poet-visionary who hears rather than invents.

In Sita Swayamvar: Valmiki is called the first poet, adi-kavi. In this scene he is a questioner before he is an author.

Sarga

A chapter within a kanda.

sharanagati शरणागति śaraṇāgati

Taking refuge; arriving at a shelter and laying down one’s defences.

Literally: Śaraṇa, shelter; āgati, arriving. The posture of Hanuman before Rama, of Vibhishana at the shore.

Trust offered before it is proven, and received as a responsibility.

Shiva’s bow शिव-धनुष śiva-dhanuṣ

The ancient bow kept by Janaka’s house, said to have belonged to Shiva.

Literally: Dhanuṣ, bow. The tradition also calls it Pināka, Shiva’s own bow.

In Sita Swayamvar: The bow is an inheritance before it is a test. Rama asks permission before touching it.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana

Skandha

A book of the Bhagavata Purana. There are twelve.

tapasya तपस्या tapasyā

Sustained inner heat: the discipline that forges a person over time.

Literally: Tap, to burn, to glow. Austerity as warmth, not punishment.

Dhruva’s months of austerity in Madhuvana; Bhagiratha bringing Ganga down; Valmiki in the anthill.

vanara

The forest people of Kishkindha, Hanuman’s people.

Vraja

The cattle-herding country around Vrindavan where Krishna grew up.

withdrawal संहार saṃhāra

The calling-back of a weapon; the knowledge of how to stop what one has started.

Literally: “Gathering back together.” Sam + hṛ, to bring in. The same word names the dissolution at the end of an age.

In Sita Swayamvar: Rama’s second question. Readiness includes the ability to end an action before it outruns its purpose.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana
